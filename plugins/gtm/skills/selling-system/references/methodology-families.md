# Methodology families — pick one spine, don't blend a soup

Discovery and qualification methodologies overlap and partly contradict. Blending five
produces a rubric no seller can run. Pick **one spine** appropriate to the deal shape,
credit it, and adapt — don't stack them.

## The families

**MEDDICC / MEDDPICC** — Metrics, Economic buyer, Decision criteria, Decision process,
Identify pain, Champion, Competition (+ Paper process). A *qualification* discipline,
not a discovery script. Best for complex, multi-stakeholder B2B deals with real
procurement. Its gift: it forces you to name the economic buyer and the decision
process, which is where most enterprise deals actually die. Weakness: says little
about *how* to run the conversation.

**SPICED (Winning by Design)** — Situation, Pain, Impact, Critical event, Decision.
A customer-led *discovery* frame built around impact and a compelling event. Best for
recurring-revenue SaaS and a customer-success-led motion. Its gift: Impact and
Critical event push past surface pain to quantified consequence and urgency. Pairs
well as the discovery layer under MEDDICC's qualification layer (this is the one
sanctioned blend, because they operate at different levels).

**Challenger** — Teach, Tailor, Take control; lead with a commercial insight that
reframes the buyer's problem. Best when the buyer doesn't yet know they have the
problem you solve, or in crowded categories. Its gift: the reframe. Modern Challenger
folds in **buyer-enablement** — equipping the buyer's internal consensus (Gartner's
finding that the average B2B buying group is ~5.4 people and mostly stuck). Directly
feeds Job 3.

**SPIN (Rackham)** — Situation, Problem, Implication, Need-payoff. The classic
question-sequencing discipline. Best as a teaching frame for *how to ask* — the
Implication questions (drawing out the cost of the problem) are still the sharpest
tool in discovery. Old but not stale.

**JOLT (Dixon, *The JOLT Effect*)** — for indecision, not objections. Judge the
indecision, Offer a recommendation, Limit the options, Take risk off the table. Best
when deals die in "no decision" rather than to a competitor. Its gift: treats the
real enemy (the status quo / fear of a wrong call) rather than pretending every lost
deal was a feature gap.

**BANT** — Budget, Authority, Need, Timing. Widely used, structurally weak: it
qualifies for the seller's convenience, not the buyer's problem, and rewards
box-ticking over diagnosis. Name it only to explain why you're not using it as the
spine. If a client insists on BANT, at least replace "Need" with a real
problem-diagnosis step.

## Pick-one-spine decision guide
- **Complex, multi-stakeholder, procurement-heavy** → MEDDICC as the qualification
  spine, SPICED or SPIN as the discovery layer beneath it.
- **Recurring-revenue SaaS, CS-led** → SPICED.
- **Buyer doesn't know they have the problem / crowded category** → Challenger, with
  buyer-enablement into Job 3.
- **Deals dying in "no decision"** → JOLT for the close, SPICED for discovery.
- **Teaching a team how to ask better** → SPIN as the discovery discipline.

State the choice and why in the output's "The call" line. One spine, adapted — that's
the discipline. Lineage credited above.
