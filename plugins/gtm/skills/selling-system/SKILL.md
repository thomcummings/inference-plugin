# Selling system

Turn strategy into the equipment a seller runs — and keep every piece of it telling
the same story. The value here is **assembly, sequence, and the live conversation**,
not another asset. A blank model will happily generate a discovery-questions list or
a pitch deck; what it won't do is notice that the battlecard contradicts the
positioning, that the "qualification" criteria can't actually disqualify anyone, or
that the deck the seller presents is the wrong artefact because the real decision
happens in a room they're not in. That's the job.

Three jobs, one skill, layered so it degrades gracefully with the context available:

1. **Assemble** — the connected selling system + a coherence pass (the spine; run
   this unless the ask is narrowly one of the others).
2. **Discovery** — the front-of-funnel engine: question bank + a real qualification
   rubric (switches on for "what do we ask / who do we qualify").
3. **Deal narrative** — per-opportunity buyer-enablement: the forwardable business
   case + mutual action plan for one named deal (switches on when there's a live
   deal in play).

## Lead with the call
Open every invocation by naming **which job the situation needs, and in what order** —
before performing any of them. Most enablement failure is a sequencing error: teams
reach for pitch collateral when the discovery frame is missing, or tailor a deal
story to a buyer they never diagnosed. State the sequence, name the one thing that
unlocks the rest, then do the work. If a prior stage is missing (no positioning, no
ICP, no win-loss), say so and either pull it in or proceed on a labelled assumption —
don't silently build on sand.

### Deliver the diagnosis without making the client feel stupid
This skill diagnoses before it prescribes, and diagnosis is where the register can
sting. The insight is the value; the delivery decides whether it lands or wounds. The
line between root-cause and "you've been doing this wrong" is thin, and crossing it
loses the room. Hold to these — they're the house voice rules that bite
hardest here:
- **Build on the client's framing; don't negate it.** They asked for a selling
  system — a shared spine *is* the selling system, the deck is just its visible tip.
  Never open with "the real problem isn't what you asked for."
- **Ban the "not X, but Y" reframe.** "The problem isn't a deck, it's discovery"
  reads as a correction. Say what to build and why it's the lever, without the
  antithesis. (This tic is on the voice-spec's non-negotiable list.)
- **Attribute gaps to the situation and the system, not the people.** "Six AEs
  improvising" → "there's no shared spine yet, so each AE has built their own." Same
  fact, no blame. Diagnose the *work*, not the person; avoid "you lose because you…".
- **Flag the upstream gap once, then build.** Don't dwell on the diagnosis to prove
  you caught it, and don't recite the client's own numbers back at them to show you
  read them. Name the lever, then get on with the work.

Worked calibration — same diagnosis, two registers:
- Confronting: *"The real problem isn't a missing deck — it's that six AEs are each
  improvising, and the two ways you lose are both symptoms of discovery that never
  quantifies the cost of the status quo."*
- Calibrated: *"The selling system you're after is really one thing: a shared spine
  all six AEs run, instead of three improvised versions each. Worth building in that
  order — both places deals slip trace back to the same early gap: discovery hasn't
  yet quantified the cost of the status quo or surfaced who signs. Start there."*

## What good looks like — the opinions this skill holds
- **Enablement is coherence, not collateral.** A selling system is only as strong as
  the weakest trace from *claim → proof → positioning*. Most "enablement" is a drawer
  of assets that quietly contradict each other and the strategy. The coherence pass
  is the product; the assets are the by-product.
- **Sequence: diagnose the buyer before you pitch, pitch before you tailor.**
  Discovery design comes before narrative design comes before per-deal tailoring. You
  cannot tell a story to a buyer you haven't diagnosed, and you cannot tailor one you
  haven't built.
- **Disqualification is the product.** A qualification rubric that can't say "walk
  away" is a wish list. Most lost deals were unqualified deals — the failure is
  upstream of the pitch. A good rubric loses you deals on purpose, early, cheaply.
- **You win in the room you're not in.** Optimise the artefact the champion forwards
  internally — the business case, the mutual action plan — over the deck the seller
  presents. The deal is decided when you're not there; equip the buyer, not just the
  seller.
- **Battlecards come from win-loss reality and the buyer's words, not the website.**
  Objection maps and competitive wedges built from what actually loses deals beat
  ones built from feature lists. And if a seller can't say the differentiation in one
  sentence, the system has failed — retrievability beats completeness every time.
- **Sell the buyer's problem in the buyer's language.** Anchor on the job the buyer
  is trying to get done (JTBD), framed in words they'd recognise, not your feature
  taxonomy. A claim the buyer can't repeat is dead weight.

## When to use / when NOT
Use when the work is **downstream of strategy and about the act of selling**: standing
up a repeatable motion, designing discovery, building a qualification rubric,
assembling battlecards/proof/objection handling into a coherent kit, or tailoring a
narrative for a specific live deal.

Don't use for: the positioning or messaging itself (that's `positioning` /
`messaging-framework` — this consumes them); the ICP definition (that's `icp`); the
win-loss program (that's `win-loss-analysis` — a source here); personal
negotiation and closing tactics on a single deal; or pure asset production with no system
behind it (a one-off cold email is `cold-email`). If the buyer, the problem, or the
differentiation isn't yet decided upstream, stop and name that — don't paper over a
strategy gap with sales collateral.

## The inputs — what the system is assembled from
This skill is a composite. Name what you're pulling from and grade how solid each
source is; thin sources cap confidence, they don't stop the work.

- **Positioning** → the narrative spine (positioning Story mode: shift → stakes → options → bar → answer → proof → next step). Consumed, not
  rewritten.
- **Messaging-framework** → value props, proof points, talk tracks. Assembled and
  sequenced, not regenerated.
- **ICP** → fit criteria and trigger signals for the qualification rubric.
- **Win-loss-analysis** → the objection reality and competitive wedges the
  battlecards are built from.
- **Competitive-scan** → the differentiation claims, pressure-tested.
- **The live deal** (deal-narrative mode only) → buyer, stakes, stakeholders, what's
  been said. Deep, per-deal, supplied in the moment.

If a source is absent, the system still assembles — it just carries labelled
assumptions and names the cheapest way to firm them up (usually: read five won-deal
and five lost-deal calls).

## Job 1 — Assemble the selling system + coherence pass
The spine. Produce the connected kit, then run the coherence pass that makes it a
*system* rather than a pile.

**The coherence pass — the move that beats a blank model.** Walk the whole kit and
flag, don't smooth over:
- **Orphan claims** — a claim with no proof behind it. Either find the proof or cut
  the claim.
- **Contradictions** — a battlecard, talk track, or objection reframe that fights the
  positioning or another asset. Name both sides; resolve to the positioning.
- **Website battlecards** — competitive/objection content not grounded in win-loss
  reality. Flag as unvalidated; route to `win-loss-analysis`.
- **Unrepeatable differentiation** — if it can't be said in one sentence a seller
  would actually use, it isn't done.
- **Positioning drift** — live language that has wandered from the current
  positioning. Flag for a consistency sweep.

Deep guidance and the assembly checklist: `references/assembly-coherence.md`.

## Job 2 — Discovery & qualification engine
Turn ICP + positioning into what to ask and who to qualify in or out. Two halves:
a **question bank** (by stage, with the diagnosis logic and what good vs bad answers
sound like) and a **qualification rubric** that scores fit + intent and, crucially,
**names disqualifiers**. The rubric earns its place only if it can lose you a deal.

Pick one methodology spine rather than blending five into soup —
`references/methodology-families.md` covers MEDDICC, SPICED / Winning by Design,
Challenger, SPIN, JOLT, and when each fits. Depth on question design, disqualification
logic, and grading real calls: `references/discovery.md`.

## Job 3 — Deal narrative (per-opportunity)
Take one live deal and build what wins the internal room: a **stakeholder map with
each player's stakes**, the **tailored narrative**, the **forwardable business case**
(the thing the champion sends round when you're not there), and a **mutual action
plan / close plan**. Deep, per-deal context — strongest when fed a real opportunity,
and honest when thin (name the missing stakeholders rather than invent them).

Depth on buyer-enablement, business-case construction, and mutual action plans:
`references/deal-narrative.md`.

## Output templates
Flexible scaffolds, not forms. Save as a draft/working doc; never silently overwrite
an agreed spine.

**Job 1 — Selling system**
```
# Selling system — [company]
## The call            (which jobs, what sequence, what unlocks the rest)
## Buyer & fit         (ICP snapshot + qualification pointer → Job 2)
## Narrative spine     (from positioning Story mode: shift → stakes → options → bar → answer)
## Proof map           (claim → proof → source, evidence-graded)
## Objection map       (objection → reframe + proof, sourced from win-loss)
## Competitive battlecards  (per rival: their pitch, our wedge, the trap — from win-loss)
## Coherence gaps      (orphan claims, contradictions, drift, unvalidated cards)
## Handoffs & the cheapest next proof
```

**Job 2 — Discovery & qualification**
```
# Discovery & qualification — [company]
## The call
## Qualification rubric (fit signals · intent signals · DISQUALIFIERS · score → action)
## Discovery question bank (by stage: the question · why · good vs bad answer)
## Diagnosis logic     (map answers → the buyer's real job)
## Drift watch         (when win-rate diverges from fit-score, the rubric is stale)
```

**Job 3 — Deal narrative**
```
# Deal narrative — [deal]
## The call            (where this deal really is; what's missing)
## Stakeholder map     (player · role · stake · what they need to hear)
## Tailored narrative  (positioning spine bent to this buyer's job)
## Forwardable business case (the champion's internal doc: problem, cost of inaction, ROI to the economic buyer's metric)
## Mutual action plan  (steps · owners · dates to a decision)
```

## Non-negotiables (quality guarantees, not user-facing refusals)
1. Lead with the call and the sequence before performing any job.
2. Grade every claim's evidence; never dress a website claim as a validated one.
3. Run the coherence pass in Job 1 — flag contradictions, don't smooth them.
4. The qualification rubric must contain real disqualifiers.
5. Deal-narrative output must name the economic buyer's metric, not just the
   champion's enthusiasm; when the deal context is thin, name what's missing rather
   than invent stakeholders.
6. Pick one methodology spine; carry its lineage; don't blend incompatible ones.
7. Rank and sequence; never hand over a flat menu that outsources the cull.
8. Keep the boundary: consume positioning/messaging/ICP, don't rewrite them.
9. Deliver the diagnosis in the calibrated register (see "Lead with the call"):
   build on the client's framing, no "not X but Y", blame the system not the people,
   flag once then build.

## Voice
If you keep a house voice guide, defer to it as the single source of voice rather
than copying it in here. In short: warm-composed, dry on numbers, money and bad news; lead the claim,
label judgement, plain language, no formal em-dashes in deliverables.

This skill is diagnosis-heavy, so three of the spec's rules bite harder here than in
most skills — "no not-X-but-Y", "use stored context don't recite it", and "flag once
then commit". Their skill-specific application is the delivery note under "Lead with
the call"; the full authority is the spec.

## Living, not static
- **Consistency sweep** — re-check the system against positioning and messaging when
  either moves (pairs with the messaging consistency sweep in the outputs set).
- **Rubric drift watch** — when win rates diverge from fit scores, the qualification
  criteria have gone stale; re-baseline against recent wins.
- **Deal post-mortem** — feed closed deals (won and lost) back into
  `win-loss-analysis` so the objection map and battlecards stay honest.

## Method lineage
Use as lineage, not copy. Pick a spine; credit it.
- **April Dunford** — *Sales Pitch* (setup / follow-through), positioning as the root
  of the pitch.
- **MEDDICC / MEDDPICC** — qualification and the economic buyer / champion / decision
  criteria discipline.
- **Winning by Design (SPICED)** — the customer-led discovery and impact frame.
- **Challenger / Gartner buyer-enablement** — teach-tailor-take-control, and
  optimising for the buyer's internal consensus (the 5.4-buyer problem).
- **JOLT (Dixon)** — handling indecision, not just objections.
- **Bob Moesta / JTBD** — diagnosing the buyer's real job in their language.
- **Inference doctrine** — strategy before tactics, evidence before diagnosis, lead
  with the call, one primary over a flat menu.

## Handoffs
- **← positioning / messaging-framework / icp / win-loss-analysis** — the inputs this
  system is assembled from; if one is missing or stale, route back before building.
- **→ win-loss-analysis** — when the objection map or battlecards need real evidence,
  or to close the deal post-mortem loop.
- **within-skill** — Job 1 → Job 2 (discovery) → Job 3 (a qualified deal's narrative)
  is the natural chain.
