# Anchoring and cadence — stretching past B2B SaaS

This skill is anchored in B2B SaaS launch experience. That's a strength (it's grounded)
and a risk (it can smuggle in assumptions that don't hold for services, physical, or
regulated products). This file separates the **durable** frame from the **swappable**
menu, and handles the most SaaS-bound idea in the skill: cadence.

## What's universal (keep)

The tier *dimensions* travel to any business:

- **Reach** — how many people this is for.
- **Investment** — how much money, time, and attention it warrants.
- **Lead time** — how far ahead the moment needs building.
- **Channel breadth** — how many surfaces fire, from "one changelog line" to "full mix".

The rubric dimensions (strategic weight, commercial impact, newness, reach, competitive
pressure, customer pull) are also universal. Score any release, in any business, against
them.

## What's software-native (swap)

Mark these as instantiated menus, not laws. When the client isn't self-serve SaaS, swap
the surface and keep the intent:

1. **Cadence assumes software ship-speed.** "Launch multiple times a month" only works
   when you ship that often. For services / physical / regulated products, cadence
   becomes **campaign rhythm**, not release rhythm — the drumbeat is themed campaigns and
   milestones (a season, a report, an event), not weekly ships. Same rolling-thunder
   logic, slower clock.
2. **Channels.** Changelog, in-app message, ProductHunt, HackerNews are software-native.
   Swap for the client's real surfaces: trade press, events, a field/partner motion, a
   retail moment, a regulatory announcement, a customer roadshow. The *breadth* per tier
   holds; the *items* change.
3. **Activation metrics (WAU / DAU)** assume PLG / self-serve. For sales-led, physical,
   or services businesses, the success metric is pipeline, bookings, qualified demand, or
   adoption measured differently. Set the metric to the model, not the SaaS default.
4. **Beta / early-access** assumes a gateable product surface. Where there isn't one, the
   "listening stage" becomes a soft pilot, a design-partner group, a limited region, or a
   preview to a customer advisory board — same job (fine-tune messaging, source proof),
   different mechanism.

## Cadence — the rolling-thunder model (and its limit)

A release is a **chapter, not a one-off.** The strongest launch programmes read as a
serialised story: each release advances an ongoing narrative, and small releases bundle
under a theme so they compound instead of scattering. This is "rolling thunder" — a
steady, connected drumbeat rather than isolated bangs.

Two practical consequences the skill should apply:

- **Bundle small items under a theme.** Don't let T3/T4 ships dribble out unconnected.
  Group them into a themed moment ("what's new in X", a seasonal release) so they add up.
- **Every release names its chapter.** In the story-fit step, say which ongoing storyline
  this release advances. A release that advances none is an orphan — flag it.

**The limit:** cadence is the most SaaS-bound idea here. High-frequency launching is a
software luxury. For anything with a slower production cycle, don't force a fake drumbeat
— translate cadence into campaign rhythm and let the tempo match how the business
actually ships. Note this explicitly rather than importing a weekly-ship assumption.

## The agentic shift (why the default moves)

In an agentic world the marginal cost of assets and channel-variants collapses. That
changes the sensible default toward: **bigger, more frequent releases, more surface area
per release, and a deliberate post-launch squeeze** — because producing the tenth variant
is nearly free. Hence the [A]/[H] flags on every asset: the plan should show what an agent
can draft so the team spends its scarce human attention on the value-prop phrasing and the
calls that matter.

But argue it, don't assume it. Cheap assets don't mean infinite attention, and a launch
firing on ten channels the audience ignores is still noise. The dividend is real; the
constraint (audience attention, brand coherence, human review capacity) is also real.
Hold both.
