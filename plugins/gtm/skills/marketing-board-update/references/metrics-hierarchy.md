# Metrics hierarchy & KPI format

Order matters. Present metrics in descending strategic importance and stop when the
board has enough to judge the commercial picture. More numbers do not buy more
credibility — a focused few that explain reality beat a dashboard dump every time.

## The four tiers

### Tier 1 — Business impact (lead here)
The board reads this first because it answers "did marketing move the business?"
- Revenue contribution (sourced and influenced).
- Pipeline contribution.
- Pipeline coverage vs target.
- Growth vs plan.
- New-logo contribution.
- Expansion contribution (if marketing affects it).

### Tier 2 — Growth quality (is it durable and efficient?)
Once impact is established, show whether it's economically sound.
- CAC.
- CAC payback.
- Cost per qualified opportunity.
- Pipeline per £/$ spent.
- Conversion rate by funnel stage.
- Win rate for marketing-originated opportunities.
- Average deal size / ACV by source or segment.
- ICP fit / account quality.
- Retention or expansion indicators, where marketing influences them.

### Tier 3 — Leading indicators (only if they explain future performance)
Include selected upstream signals that predict Tier 1/2 movement — not for their
own sake.
- Demo / qualified-meeting volume.
- Qualified lead volume.
- Website conversion rate.
- Branded search trend.
- Content engagement *tied to pipeline*.
- Event-sourced meetings.

### Tier 4 — Activity metrics (appendix, mostly)
Not useless, but they must not anchor the narrative unless one of them predicts a
material business outcome.
- Impressions, clicks, email opens.
- Social follower growth.
- Content output.
- MQL totals without a quality context.

## Breakdowns worth showing (Tier 1/2)
Where it sharpens the story, break contribution out by: segment, geography, ICP
tier, new-logo vs expansion, inbound vs outbound-assisted. The point is to show
marketing affects commercial throughput, not just awareness — so pick the cut that
reveals that.

## KPI scorecard format
Every headline KPI carries its context. Never a bare number.

| Metric | Current | Prior | Target | Variance | Read |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

The "Read" cell is one line of interpretation — the "so what" — not a restatement of
the number. Show 3–5 headline KPIs, not fifteen.

## Doc vs appendix
- **Main doc:** Tier 1, the few Tier 2 measures that explain durability, and only
  the Tier 3 signals that explain what's coming.
- **Appendix:** channel-level breakdowns, full funnel tables, attribution
  methodology notes, campaign-level performance, content performance detail,
  experiment backlog. The appendix proves command of detail if challenged; it
  should never dominate the meeting.

## How many items, total
A board update usually carries: 3–5 headline KPIs, 2–4 strategic bets, 1–3 key
risks, 1–3 asks. More than that and marketing reads as unfocused.
