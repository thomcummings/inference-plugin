# Board language & reframing

Boards evaluate revenue impact, repeatability, efficiency, management judgement,
risk, and resource allocation. So the update speaks investor language, not internal
team language. This file holds the translations and worked reframes.

## Prefer / avoid
Lead with the commercial register:

- Pipeline and revenue contribution.
- CAC and payback.
- Conversion rates and win rate.
- Segment / ICP quality.
- Retention and expansion implications.
- Strategic bets, risks and mitigation, decisions needed.

Avoid leading with: number of campaigns launched, content volume, social
engagement, impressions, traffic without commercial context, isolated channel
anecdotes, or team busyness as proof of progress.

## Translation table
| Instead of… | Say… |
|---|---|
| Lead gen | Pipeline creation |
| Media performance | Acquisition efficiency |
| Funnel engagement | Commercial conversion |
| Lead quality (when it's about revenue) | Growth quality |
| Campaign | Strategic bet |
| Budget split | Capital allocation |
| "We feel / we think" | "The signal / evidence suggests" |

Avoid language that sounds tactical, busy, or self-congratulatory. Confident and
declarative beats breathless.

## Reframing examples (weak → strong)
Each pairs a tactical line with the commercially-framed version. The pattern:
outcome first, comparison implied, implication stated.

**1.**
- Weak: "Published 12 new pieces of content and ran two virtual events."
- Strong: "Mid-funnel content and virtual events generated a higher share of
  qualified pipeline this quarter, supporting expansion in the core ICP without
  raising blended acquisition cost."

**2.**
- Weak: "Improved paid social CPC by 21%."
- Strong: "Paid social efficiency improved, but more importantly pipeline per pound
  of spend rose, making the channel a more viable scaling lever next quarter."

**3.**
- Weak: "Updated positioning and refreshed the homepage."
- Strong: "Positioning was narrowed to match enterprise buying criteria; early
  evidence shows higher qualification and better sales conversations, with some
  reduction in low-fit inbound."

**4.**
- Weak: "We hired an agency and rebuilt landing pages."
- Strong: "Conversion infrastructure was upgraded to raise yield from existing
  demand and lower the cost of pipeline creation over time."

## The "so what" filter
After every metric or update, add the implication. Three shapes that work:
- Reallocation: "…so spend is moving toward higher-intent channels."
- Risk surfaced: "…which increases paid dependency risk next quarter."
- Efficiency gained: "…reducing unqualified inbound and improving sales efficiency."
