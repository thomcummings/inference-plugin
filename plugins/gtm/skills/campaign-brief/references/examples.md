# Worked examples — one good brief, one bad one

Both fictional. The good brief shows the standard working; the bad one is annotated with
its rubric violations — it's the more instructive of the two, because it fails the way real
briefs fail: politely, plausibly, and by committee.

---

## Example 1 — a good brief (annotated)

# "The Returns Tax" report launch — brief
**Status:** agreed · **Owner:** Head of Marketing · **Approver:** CEO · **Date:** 3 Feb

## Why this, why now
Q1 diagnosis: we win when buyers understand returns as a cost centre, not a shipping
problem [audit §4]. No asset makes that argument at scale. The plan's W2 (authority)
carries 30% of this year's pipeline gap [plan]. This is its flagship move.

## Objective — the one change
**150 report downloads by ICP-fit e-commerce operators (50–500k orders/yr) by 31 March,
of which 30 become open sales conversations.**
*(Note the shape: one number pair, dated, and qualified by fit — not "downloads". The
second clause is the objective's teeth; the first is its leading edge.)*

## Audience — who, exactly
The operations lead at a DTC brand doing 50–500k orders/yr, personally fielding the CFO's
question about why refund costs rose 20% while sales grew 8%. They believe returns are
"the cost of doing DTC" — the campaign must break that resignation.
*(Picturable person, named belief to work against, trigger moment. Not "e-commerce
businesses".)*

## Proposition — the single-minded thing
**"You're paying a returns tax you can itemise — and stop paying."**
Supports (subordinate): benchmark data from 400 stores; the 5-line itemisation framework;
what the top decile does differently.
*(One repeatable sentence. A colleague could relay it. Supports capped at three.)*

## Channels & assets
Report (owner: agency, 28 Feb) · landing page with "how did you hear" field (growth mkt,
1 Mar) · founder LinkedIn series, 6 posts from the data (founder + HoM, w/c 3 Mar) ·
partner newsletter swaps ×3 (HoM, March) · sales one-pager (HoM, 7 Mar).
Channels trace to the channel map: founder LinkedIn and partnerships are Build-firsts;
no paid until organic proves the hook [CM].

## Flighting & budget
Launch moment w/c 3 Mar (report + founder series + partner sends stacked); echo phase
through March (data cuts as posts, sales enablement in live deals). £3.2k total: £2.5k
agency production, £700 design. Team cost: ~6 days HoM, capped.
*(A moment plus echoes, not a drizzle. What the team is NOT doing while this runs: the
podcast pauses for March [plan refusals].)*

## Success, defined now
Judging: 30 fit-qualified conversations opened by 30 April (baseline: ~12/mo all-source).
Leading: 150 downloads by 31 Mar; ≥40% of downloads ICP-fit (guardrail — a viral report
full of students is a miss). Kill/iterate checkpoint: <50 downloads by 14 Mar → the hook
is wrong; re-cut the angle before spending the partner sends.
*(Verdict number, steering numbers, a guardrail, and a dated decision point with the
action named.)*

## Risks & dependencies
Benchmark data cleared for publication (legal, by 21 Feb) — early signal: draft clause
this week. Founder posting appetite holds for 6 posts — mitigation: batch-record in one
session. Partner sends land in March windows — confirm by 28 Feb.

## Example 2 — a bad brief (annotated with violations)

# "Q2 Brand Awareness Campaign" — brief
**Owner:** "marketing" · **Approver:** not stated

> ❌ **Rubric 4 warning, immediately:** no named owner, no approver — nobody's neck is in
> this brief.

## Objective
Increase brand awareness and drive more traffic to the website, while supporting the sales
team with better leads and building our social presence.

> ❌ **Rubric 1 (one objective): four objectives, none measurable, none dated.**
> "Awareness" is not a change in the world; it's a category of hope. Each stakeholder's
> wish got appended rather than argued. The fix isn't wordsmithing — it's the argument
> the team hasn't had: *which one thing, this quarter?*

## Audience
E-commerce businesses of all sizes who could benefit from better returns management, plus
agencies, partners, and investors who should know about us.

> ❌ **Rubric 2 (one audience): that's a TAM statement plus bystanders.** "All sizes" and
> "plus investors" means the copy will be written for nobody. No belief to work against,
> no trigger moment — nothing for creative to grip.

## Key messages
- Industry-leading returns management platform
- AI-powered insights and seamless integrations
- Trusted by hundreds of brands
- Great customer support and fast onboarding

> ❌ **Rubric 3 (one proposition): feature soup.** Four bullets, all category claims any
> competitor could paste into their own deck. Nothing repeatable, nothing believed-in.
> Also a quiet **Rubric 7 violation**: "industry-leading" and "AI-powered" appear nowhere
> in the company's positioning doc — the brief is inventing claims.

## Channels
LinkedIn, Twitter/X, TikTok, YouTube, email, blog, PR, podcast, paid social, events.

> ❌ **Rubric 5 (channel–audience match): that's every channel, which is no channel.**
> No trace to where the audience actually is; TikTok appears because someone senior
> mentioned it (**Rubric 6**: nothing here is costed, so effort–ambition can't even be
> checked). ❌ **Rubric 8**: everything launches at once, nothing is planned for week two.

## Measurement
We'll track engagement, impressions, reach, and followers, and review performance at the
end of the quarter.

> ❌ **Rubric 4 (success defined before launch): all four metrics are vanity for this
> purpose** — each can double while pipeline stands still. "Review at the end" means the
> kill/iterate decision arrives after the money is gone. No baseline, no threshold, no
> checkpoint, no guardrail.

### The review verdict this brief would receive

**Rethink.** It fails on objective, audience AND proposition — fixing channels and
metrics won't save a campaign that doesn't know what change it wants in whose mind. The
restarting question: *"If this campaign works, what specifically happens in April that
isn't happening now — and who did it?"* Answer that in one sentence, and the brief can be
rewritten in an afternoon.
