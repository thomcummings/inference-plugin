# Check: is it a positioning problem?

Before building, find out whether positioning is really what's wrong, which kind of
problem it is, and whether now is the time. Plenty of go-to-market pain looks like a
positioning problem and isn't: a weak offer, the wrong channel, a sales team that doesn't
qualify, or a product that isn't ready.

## 1. Gather the current story

Ask for:

- The current one-liner, as the sales team actually says it.
- The homepage headline and the category the company puts itself in.
- The happiest customers, and what they have in common.
- The last five wins and the last five losses, with one line on why for each.
- How each function describes what makes the product different.

Write these down side by side. Gaps between them are often the first finding.

## 2. Look for symptoms

Mark each one Present, Partly or Absent, and note the evidence and its grade (Seen, Said
or Supposed).

**In the sales conversation**
- Prospects ask the rep to start again, or summarise the product back wrongly.
- Prospects file the product under the wrong category, then judge it against the wrong
  rivals and the wrong price.
- "Couldn't we just do this ourselves?" comes up often.
- The moment a prospect gets it arrives late, on the second or third call.
- Many qualified deals stall and end with no purchase at all.
- Sales cycles feel long for the deal size, and the delay is understanding, not process.

**Inside the company**
- Founders, product, sales and success each describe the product differently.
- New hires take months to explain what the company does with any confidence.
- The homepage has been rewritten several times without moving conversion.
- Campaigns aim at "everyone in the industry" because nobody can say who they're for.

**In the market**
- The product has changed a lot since the positioning was last set.
- The best customers today aren't the ones the positioning was written for.
- A rival has repositioned, or a new kind of alternative has appeared.
- Buyers' expectations of the category have moved, for example because AI changed what
  "good" looks like.

## 3. Name the problem type

| Type | What's going on | Main tell | What to do |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Split story** | The founder can tell the story, but the team tells several | Internal symptoms dominate; the pitch varies by rep | Capture what the founder knows and spread it. A short alignment session and a light Build may be enough |
| **Stale** | The product or market has moved and the positioning hasn't | Market symptoms dominate; new capabilities or new best-fit customers | Full Build, with fresh eyes on fallbacks and edges |
| **Inherited** | Nobody ever chose the positioning; it came with the founding idea | The category was assumed, not chosen; the product is described as what it was meant to be | Full Build, with extra time on letting go of the original category |
| **Too early** | There aren't yet enough happy customers to see a pattern | Fewer than about 10 paying customers; use cases vary widely; the product changes every sprint | Don't run a Build yet. Keep the positioning broad and watch which customers love it |

Problems can combine, and Stale and Split story often appear together. Name the main one and
note the others.

**Keeping it broad while it's too early.** A narrow early position that turns out wrong is
expensive to unwind. Describe the problem you solve in broad terms, sell to whoever feels
it, and watch for the cluster of customers who buy fast, use it hard and tell others. When
three to five of them share traits, it's time to Build.

## 4. Check the timing

Good time to Build:
- Enough paying customers to see who loves the product and why (roughly 10 to 20 or more).
- A clear pattern among the happiest ones.
- Visible go-to-market friction from mixed stories.
- A moment that demands clarity: a raise, a rebrand, a new market, a merger, a launch.

Too early, or the wrong time:
- Private beta, or still changing what the core product is.
- Too few customers to see a pattern.
- No evidence yet that anyone will pay.
- A pivot is under discussion. Settle that first.

## 5. Recommend

- **Split story:** an alignment session, a light canvas, and a plan to spread it (sales
  enablement, homepage, onboarding for new hires).
- **Stale or Inherited:** a full Build. Say who needs to be involved, what evidence to
  gather first (win/loss notes, five to ten customer interviews, usage data) and roughly how
  long it will take.
- **Too early:** defer. Suggest customer development instead and say what signal would
  trigger a Build.
- **Not a positioning problem:** say so plainly, and say what the problem looks like instead
  (offer, channel, qualification, product).
