# Synthesis structure (Phase 4, internal register)

The synthesis turns the per-competitor evidence into a picture the human can
pattern-match against. It **surfaces** findings and candidates — it does not hand
down strategic conclusions. Open with a short "how to read this" note that says so,
and label it agent-authored, draft for review.

Use this section order.

## 0. How to read this (framing note)

2–4 sentences: what this is, that it surfaces findings rather than concluding, that
self-claims are kept separate from corroboration, and the method/limits (e.g. "desk
research only; live social and review counts are the softest points").

## 1. The market at a glance

- A line or two on how the set divides (e.g. into jobs-to-be-done camps) and where
  the **subject company** sits within that.
- The **strategic comparison matrix** (`comparison-axes.md`, Grid 1), subject
  included as a column, standouts bolded, self-claims footnoted.
- The **feature / capability grid** (`comparison-axes.md`, Grid 2) — features as
  rows, competitors + subject as columns, consistent ✓/~/✗/? states. This is the
  buyer's-eye view and the centrepiece of the optional interactive artefact.

## 2. Per competitor: strengths / weaknesses / inconsistencies

For each competitor, three short bullet lists (bullets, not prose — they're
scannable and comparable):

- **Strengths** — what they're genuinely good at, relative to the market and the
  subject's ICP, with evidence.
- **Weaknesses** — real gaps, with evidence.
- **Inconsistencies / credibility flags** — contradictions in their own claims
  (pricing that differs by page, rating cited two ways, scale numbers that don't
  reconcile, positioning that fights the product). These double as credibility
  signals and potential attack surfaces, which is why they earn their own bucket.

## 3. Cross-cutting patterns

The market-level signals that cut across the whole set — the things most worth the
human's read. Each as a short titled paragraph. Where a pattern connects to other
work the subject already has (an audit, a channels map), note the link.

## 4. Opportunities for the subject company — candidates

Each opportunity as: the candidate, the evidence behind it, and **the open question
that would decide it**. These are candidates for the human's assessment, not
recommendations. Resist the urge to rank or commit — that's the human's pass.

## 5. Threats to the subject company

Surfaced for the human's read, roughly ordered by potential impact, each with the
evidence behind it.

## 6. Open questions the scan can't answer

The things desk research can't settle — for the human, or to put to the client/
founder. Distinguish "needs a data pull" from "needs a judgment call".

## 7. What changed since [date] (refresh mode only)

New entrants, positioning shifts, new proof, pricing moves, narrative changes since
the prior scan. Often the most valuable section for a returning client.

---

## Tone notes

- Hedge soft data honestly: "appear to", "on this level of analysis", "low
  confidence". Overclaiming on thin signals erodes trust in the solid findings.
- Keep strengths/weaknesses/inconsistencies as **bullets**; keep patterns and
  opportunities as **short paragraphs** (they carry reasoning).
- Name contradictions plainly; don't smooth messy evidence into a tidy story.
