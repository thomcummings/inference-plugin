# Evidence discipline

The credibility of a competitive scan rests entirely on how it handles evidence. A
scan that can't tell a marketing claim from a fact is just laundered marketing.
These rules apply to every research file and carry through to the synthesis.

## 1. Claims vs corroboration

Always distinguish:

- **Self-claim** — anything the competitor asserts about itself: scale numbers,
  customer counts, "the #1 / first / only", outcome stats (e.g. "saves X hours a
  week", "cuts cost by Y%"). Treat as marketing until corroborated.
- **Independent corroboration** — a third party confirming it: a review platform, a
  press piece, an analyst roundup, a directory, a verifiable ranking.

In the text, make the status legible: *"Acme claims 4.6M records (self-reported)"*
vs *"ranked #1 of 7 in [independent category roundup] (verified, dated)"*. When a
self-claim is all you have, say so — don't promote it to fact by omission.

## 2. Confidence ratings

Tag key data points **High / Med / Low**:

- **High** — directly observed on the source, or independently corroborated.
- **Med** — single credible source, or a third-party aggregator.
- **Low** — self-reported only, an estimate, or an aggregator with opaque method.

The softest points in most scans: live social follower counts, current review
counts, and third-party traffic estimates. Mark them Low unless directly verified.

## 3. Gap-flagging

End every research file with an explicit **gaps** list — what you couldn't find or
verify. Gaps are not failures; an honest gap list is what lets the human trust the
rest. Never paper over a gap with a plausible-sounding guess.

## 4. Source hygiene

- **Inline citations.** Every non-obvious claim carries its source URL where it's
  made, not just in a bibliography.
- **Sources list.** End each file with every URL used.
- **Date-stamp.** Note the scan date and "as of [month/year]" on anything
  time-sensitive (pricing, counts, rankings) — these drift fast.
- **Recency.** Prefer current pages over cached/old ones; note when a figure is
  visibly stale.

## 5. The amplification test (for the verify pass)

Before any claim makes it into a client deliverable or a marketing asset,
double-check it against its source. The claims most likely to be quoted (an
external #1 ranking, a competitor's verbatim self-description, a damning review
theme) are exactly the ones that must be solid. One verified external ranking is
worth more than ten unverified self-claims — make sure the ones you lean on are real.
