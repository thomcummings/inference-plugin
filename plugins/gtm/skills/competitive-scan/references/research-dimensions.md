# Research dimensions (Phase 3)

Each competitor is read across four dimensions. Capture verbatim where the wording
matters (positioning especially), attribute every non-obvious claim to a source,
and keep the competitor's self-claims separate from independent corroboration (see
`evidence-discipline.md`).

---

## Dimension 1 — Positioning & messaging

What the competitor *says*. Capture from the homepage and key pages (pricing,
product/features, about, any per-persona landing pages). Quote key lines verbatim
and attribute to the page.

- **Headline / H1, sub-headline, tagline** — verbatim.
- **Primary value proposition** — the core promise, in their words.
- **Category language** — how they describe what they are ("intelligence platform",
  "PR software", "AI-native…"). Note if it shifts by page/audience.
- **Named use-cases / personas** — who they explicitly target.
- **Pricing & packaging** — model, tiers, actual prices, what's gated. Note
  transparency (public vs demo-gated).
- **Trial / onboarding mechanics** — free trial length, credit-card requirement,
  demo-gating, freemium, one-time/lifetime deals.
- **On-site proof** — logos, testimonials, review badges, headline stats/claims
  (database size, customer counts), guarantees.
- **Tone / register** and **messaging hierarchy** — what they lead with vs bury.

## Dimension 2 — Owned & social presence

Channels the competitor *controls*.

- **Social accounts** — LinkedIn, X, YouTube, Instagram, etc.: follower counts,
  posting cadence/recency, themes, engagement signal. Which are active vs dormant.
- **Individual / personal distribution** — is an individual (founder, executive,
  prominent practitioner) running a personal audience or newsletter that drives real
  reach for the company? This is a major channel in some categories (early-stage and
  indie software, creator/agency, personality-led services) and minor or absent in
  others (most enterprise, consumer-brand, and established-services categories). Only
  flag it where there's visible evidence it matters.
- **Owned website / SEO surface** — comparison/alternative pages they publish,
  programmatic/templated page clusters, free tools, directories they run.

## Dimension 3 — Earned visibility & reputation

Where the competitor shows up *without controlling the narrative*. This is the
dimension that most reveals real traction vs claimed traction — invest in it. The
*surfaces* below vary completely by industry; calibrate them to the category before
researching (a SaaS tool, a law firm, a CPG brand and a field-service company share
almost none of the same earned surfaces).

- **AI-answer / AEO presence** — do they appear in AI Overviews / ChatGPT /
  Perplexity answers for category queries? What AI-native or AI-assisted positioning
  do they claim? *(Developer/tech products only: also check for an MCP server, agent
  surface, or llms.txt. Skip these for non-developer categories — they won't apply.)*
- **Organic search footprint** — what they rank for; branded vs non-branded;
  whether others' "alternatives"/"best of" lists include or omit them.
- **Press / earned media** — calibrate to the category: tech/SaaS skews to launch
  coverage and podcast/guest appearances; professional services to analyst coverage,
  rankings and awards; consumer to retail-trade press and product reviews; regulated
  fields to certifications and accreditations.
- **Review / rating surfaces** — use the surfaces that matter for the category:
  G2/Capterra/Product Hunt for software; Clutch/Chambers/industry directories for
  professional services; Trustpilot/Amazon/retail-marketplace reviews for consumer
  products; Google Business/Yelp for local and field service. Capture ratings **and**
  counts. Absence is a finding — but only flag absence from a surface that's actually
  relevant to the category.
- **Directories / roundups** — category "best of" lists; where they rank in
  independent comparisons.
- **Community sentiment** — the forums and communities that matter for the category
  (e.g. Reddit/HN for developer tools; trade forums and professional networks for
  B2B services; consumer review communities for consumer products). Praise and
  criticism themes.

## Dimension 4 — Content

What they publish and who it's for.

- **Blog / resources** — topics, cadence, recency, formats (how-to, data report,
  comparison, thought leadership).
- **Signature content assets** — proprietary data reports ("state of…"), recurring
  rankings, free tools, newsletters, podcasts, video series, webinar programmes, or
  whatever recurring formats the competitor actually runs. Only include formats they
  genuinely use; don't go looking for a podcast (or any format) by default.
- **Content strategy & target reader** — what the content is optimised for (SEO
  intent, lead-gen, brand) and *who it actually attracts* vs who the product
  targets. A mismatch here is a high-value finding.
