# Phase 4 — Operating system (run it)

The model is only real if it runs. This phase turns the architecture, voices and
distribution into something a team (or a solo operator, or an agent system) executes
week after week. Components: the **roadmap** (the backlog), **cadence**, the **production
workflow**, the **owner/RACI model**, and **measurement**.

## 1. The content roadmap: full field guide

The roadmap is the model's backlog — one row per planned piece. The fields make the four
tests (Purpose, Position, Provenance, Propagation) concrete and auditable. Group them:

**Brief**
| Field | What it captures | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Content type | the format template (from the Phase 3 map) | POV essay |
| Signature topic | which standing claim it advances | AI-native marketing |
| Anchor it feeds | the hub it collates up to | "AI-native function" essay/index |
| Primary segment / ICP | who it's aimed at | senior in-house marketing leaders |
| Funnel stage | where it sits | Awareness |
| Primary channel(s) | where it goes first (distribution-first) | site + newsletter |
| Gated / CTA / magnet? | its conversion job, if any | ungated; CTA to Blueprint |
| Primary creative | the core visual/asset | layer-cake diagram |

**Voice & production**
| Field | What it captures | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Human voice / owner | which roster voice carries it | founder |
| Author / producer | who makes it | founder + agent draft |
| Format | the concrete shape | 1,500-word essay |
| Other resources | design, video, data help needed | carousel design |
| Reviewers | who signs off | — |

**Prioritisation & scope**
| Field | What it captures | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Planned quarter / month | when | Q3 |
| Size / effort | cost to publish | 2 days |
| Potential impact | leads or reach upside | High |
| OKR it supports | the business goal | grow qualified inbound |
| Storyline / pillar it supports | the storyline it proves | "marketing is being rebuilt" |

**Propagation & proof**
| Field | What it captures | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Fragment / atomisation plan | what it repackages into | carousel, thread, 3 clips |
| Un-fakeable core | which of POV/proof/personality it carries | POV + receipts (real rebuild) |
| Measurement | what it's for + leading/lagging signal | leading: saves/shares; lagging: inbound |

A row that can't fill Topic, Segment, Funnel stage, Voice and Un-fakeable core is not
ready — that's the placement + moat gates enforced at the backlog level.

## 2. The prioritisation rubric

Prioritise by **impact × effort against the OKRs**, not by whoever shouts loudest.

- **Anchors earn their slot by the spider-web they seed.** A high-effort report or show
  is justified by the volume of regular pieces and fragments it will spawn and the
  credibility it concentrates — not by a single piece's reach.
- **Balance the backlog** across signature topics (so no topic goes quiet), funnel
  stages (so you're not all-awareness or all-bottom), and voices (so no single person is
  the whole model).
- **Sequence for compounding.** Build the anchor before the spider-web that points to it;
  seed the voice before scaling its lane.
- **"Nothing goes dark"** is the constraint: a sustainable minimum in each Build-first
  channel beats heroic bursts followed by silence.

## 3. Cadence patterns

Different shapes, different rhythms. Set a cadence per format and hold it.

| Format | Typical cadence | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Anchor (report/essay/show launch) | monthly → quarterly | the monuments; heavy, infrequent |
| Regular piece | weekly → fortnightly | the evergreen web; the backbone |
| Fragment / short-form | most days, per active voice | where authority compounds; native |
| Newsletter | weekly or fortnightly | the cadence *is* the commitment |
| Podcast / show episode | weekly → monthly | a franchise with a predictable slot |
| Anchor refresh | 2–4×/year | keep the hubs current; re-collate new pieces |

The discipline that separates models that work from ones that stall is showing up in the
same few places on a predictable rhythm over a long horizon. Optimise for nothing going
dark, not for any single piece popping.

## 4. The production workflow

A repeatable pipeline every piece runs:

```
Brief ──▶ Produce ──▶ Review ──▶ Publish ──▶ Distribute ──▶ Repackage ──▶ Measure ──▶ Feed back
 │          │           │          │            │             │            │           │
 roadmap    AI-assisted  moat gate  native to    native per    per the      leading/    into next
 row        draft +      + voice    primary      channel       graph        lagging     brief +
            human core   check      surface      (not copy-    (Phase 3)    signal       priorities
                                                  paste)
```

Two gates are mandatory in Review: the **moat gate** (does it carry an un-fakeable core?)
and the **voice check** (is it in the right roster voice, laddering to the narrative?).
A piece that fails either goes back or gets cut — publishing it dilutes the model.

## 5. Owners and RACI

Name, per format and per channel: who's **Responsible** (makes it), **Accountable**
(owns the outcome), **Consulted** (SMEs, the voice whose name is on it), **Informed**.
Include the **human voices** explicitly — a founder-fronted piece has the founder as an
accountable voice even if an agent or ghost drafts it. Set the operating cadence too:
weekly (pipeline + fragments), monthly (performance + roadmap review), quarterly
(strategic review — what's working, what to drop, what to expand). Default to making the
call and documenting it, not blocking.

## 6. The measurement model

Measure what the model is *for*, not just what's easy to count.

- **Leading vs lagging.** Leading: saves, shares, replies, watch-through, subscriber
  growth, "sends" to colleagues. Lagging: inbound, pipeline influenced, branded search,
  share of voice on the signature topics.
- **Audience-relationship metrics, not just traffic.** A media brand is measured by a
  *returning* audience — subscribers, repeat viewers, a growing owned list — more than by
  sessions from search. Track the relationship, not only the reach.
- **The storyline check.** The point of the signature topics is that people repeat
  them. Periodically test: are prospects, press and peers saying the storylines back to
  you? That's the truest lagging signal that the model is working.
- **Per-piece job.** Each roadmap row named a job and a signal; review against it. Kill
  content types that never hit their job; double down on the ones that do.
- **Drift watch.** Watch for the two failure modes creeping back: the library (rising
  volume, flat or falling engagement, no voice) and random acts (lots of activity, low
  ladder-rate, topics blurring). Either triggers a model review, not just more posting.

## 7. Handoff: content-OS or client team

- **Own brand:** wire the roadmap and cadence into the existing content operating system —
  its pipeline stages, its distribution steps, its anti-themes. The model is the strategy
  layer above the OS's execution; don't duplicate the OS.
- **Client:** hand the model to the client's editorial/social/video/email
  functions with the owner/RACI and cadence attached. Keep the strategic spine (narrative,
  topics, voice roster) as *propose-don't-overwrite*; let the roadmap and working docs
  accrete. Hand specific claims to `messaging-framework` and keyword depth to
  `content-strategy` rather than absorbing them here.
