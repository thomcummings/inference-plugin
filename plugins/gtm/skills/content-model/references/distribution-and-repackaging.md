# Phase 3 — Distribution & repackaging (how it travels)

The floor gives you the right pieces; this phase gets them to an audience and makes each
one earn its keep across surfaces. Two components: the **distribution model** (the
channel mesh, distribution-first) and the **repackaging / atomisation graph**. It
*consumes* the channel map — it does not re-decide which channels to back.

## 1. Distribution-first

The old order was create → then distribute. Invert it: **decide the surface and the
audience before you create.** Distribution is the first constraint, not the last step. A
piece conceived for a surface (a LinkedIn-native carousel, a YouTube-first explainer, a
search-intent guide) beats a piece created in the abstract and retrofitted to channels.
Practically: every roadmap row (Phase 4) names its primary channel *before* production
starts, and the format follows the channel.

## 2. The channel mesh vs hub-and-spoke

The 2020 architecture was hub-and-spoke: create the anchor on your site, distribute
spokes outward, drive everyone *back* to the hub. That model assumed the website was the
destination and search was the road. Today it's a **mesh**: discovery happens in-feed,
through people, and inside AI answer engines, as well as via search. Reframe the
website's role — it's a **system of record and a conversion surface**, not the place
attention lives. Content is often consumed entirely on the platform (a carousel read in
LinkedIn, a video watched on YouTube) and never "clicks through". Design for value
delivered on the surface, with the site as where intent gets captured and converted.

This does **not** mean abandon owned properties — the anchors and the media brand still
live on surfaces you control. It means stop assuming every piece's job is to route to
your domain.

## 3. The four discovery plays (and the softened SEO stance)

Treat these as **parallel plays, each with a role** — not either/or, and not one gutting
the others:

- **Search / SEO.** Still captures real demand where buyers actively search a topic —
  especially consideration and bottom-funnel queries. The pillar page is one valid anchor
  form. What changed: SEO is no longer the *sole organising logic* of the whole model,
  and zero-click + AI answers mean informational top-funnel search returns less traffic
  than it did. Back it where the buyer searches; don't build the architecture around it.
- **AEO / GEO (AI answer engines).** Visibility inside ChatGPT/Claude/Perplexity answers.
  Increasingly where informational discovery goes. Needs structured, quotable,
  well-attributed content and technical fundamentals (hand to `website-audit` in AEO scope). A growing
  play, not yet a standalone engine for most.
- **Native social / in-feed.** Where authority is built now (Phase 2's voices live here).
  Reach is driven by the platform and the person, not your domain. Primary for awareness
  and for the voice architecture.
- **Creator / borrowed reach.** Partners, guests, features, swaps — access to audiences
  you don't own yet. Highest-leverage early, relationship-led (long fuse).

Which of these to *back*, and in what order, is the channel map's call — read it and
inherit it. This phase decides how content is shaped for whichever plays are Build-first.

## 4. The repackaging / atomisation graph

One insight is raw material, not a single output. Design the graph up front so a unit of
thinking earns its keep across surfaces. A worked shape:

```
        Research / lived experience (the raw material)
                    │
        Anchor: definitive essay or report
                    │
        ┌───────────┼───────────┬─────────────┬──────────────┐
   LinkedIn      Thread       Newsletter    Conference    Podcast
   carousel                    section        talk         angle
        │                                                    │
     Clips / quote-cards / hooks (fragments) ────────────────┘
                    │
        Sales one-pager / enablement (bottom-funnel reuse)
```

Not every piece runs the whole graph — flagships do, regular pieces do a subset. The
point is deliberate reuse, decided at planning time, not improvised after publishing.
Repackaging is where lean teams and solo operators get their leverage, and where AI helps
most — it lowers the cost of *genuine* per-platform adaptation.

## 5. Native adaptation, not copy-paste

The failure mode of repackaging is amputation: the same text pasted across five
platforms. Each surface has its own grammar — a carousel is designed, not screenshotted;
a thread is paced for the scroll; a YouTube script front-loads differently than an essay;
a clip needs a hook in the first second. AI makes real adaptation cheap, so there's no
excuse for copy-paste. The moat gate (Phase 2) travels with the fragment: an adapted
piece must still carry POV, proof or personality, or it's slop at a new aspect ratio.

## 6. The funnel × format × channel × owner map

The deliverable of this phase is a table that makes distribution concrete. One row per
recurring content type:

| Content type | Funnel stage | Primary channel(s) | Native format | Voice / owner | Repackages into |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| POV essay | Awareness/Consideration | site + newsletter | long-form | founder | carousel, thread, clips |
| Podcast episode | Awareness | YouTube + podcast | video/audio | founder + guest | clips, quote-cards, newsletter |
| How-to / framework | Consideration | search + LinkedIn | article + carousel | SME | carousel, thread |
| Case study | Decision | site + sales | long-form + one-pager | brand + CS | sales enablement, quote |
| Short-form POV | Awareness | LinkedIn / X | native post | roster (varies) | — (primary) |

Adapt the rows to the business. The map is the bridge from the model to the roadmap
(Phase 4): each row becomes a repeating template the roadmap instantiates.

## 7. The anti-slop guardrail

Volume is cheap now, which makes restraint the discipline. Guardrails:
- **The moat gate applies to every surface**, including fragments and repackages.
- **More adaptation, not more copies.** Ten native pieces beat one piece posted ten ways.
- **Nothing goes dark beats one channel popping** — but "nothing goes dark" is a floor of
  *quality-passing* content, not a quota met with filler.
- **If AI produced it end-to-end with no human core, it doesn't ship.** The system
  accelerates the work; it doesn't replace the judgement that makes it worth reading.
