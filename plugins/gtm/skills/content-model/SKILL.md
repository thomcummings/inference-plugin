# Content model

This skill answers the question between strategy and the editorial calendar:

> How does this brand turn what it believes into a content-and-comms system that
> compounds — instead of a stream of disconnected posts?

It designs content the way `growth-model` designs growth: as a system, before any
tactic. The output is a *model*, not a calendar — an architecture that ladders every
piece to one narrative, a way each piece travels across the funnel and the channels,
a roster of human voices that carry it, and an operating system that keeps it running.

Works equally for a client engagement and for the user's own brand.

## The core idea: floor and overlay

Two things have to be true at once, and most teams only manage one.

**The floor is structural rigour — and it's non-negotiable.** The hierarchy of
authority (topics → anchors → pieces → fragments) and the funnel/segment mapping are
the load-bearing structure. They are not a 2020 relic; they are the thing that stops
content being *random acts of marketing wearing a content-brand costume*. Skip the
floor and you get lots of motion — personalities posting, short-form everywhere — that
never compounds into anything ownable.

**The overlay is the 2026 media-shape — and it's what makes the floor matter now.**
Content today behaves like independent media, not corporate publishing: the unit is a
media property with a host and an audience relationship; the narrative is carried by a
roster of human voices, not the logo; distribution is the first constraint, not the
last step; and because AI can generate infinite competent content, the only durable
value is the stuff a model can't fake — point of view, proof, personality, taste.

The skill exists to force **both**, and to be honest that most teams are fleeing one
failure straight into the other:

- **The old failure — the soulless library.** Architecturally tidy, SEO-driven,
  voiceless, undistributed, and now out-produced by any model in seconds.
- **The new failure — random acts of marketing.** Real personality and short-form
  energy, but no architecture, no funnel logic, no throughline; nothing accrues.

The thesis, pinned to the floor: **structure is the floor, the media-shape is the
overlay; the product is the taste, the receipts and the relationship — but only when
it stands on a real architecture, not vibes.**

## Posture

The value is the *linkage*, in both directions. Down: every piece traces to an anchor,
a signature topic, and ultimately one narrative. Forward: every piece has a job — a
funnel stage, a segment, an outcome. If a proposed piece can't be traced both ways,
the honest move is to cut it, not calendar it.

Constructive and opinionated. The skill can say "this is a calendar, not a model", or
"these five topics are four too many", or "this piece is something a model could write,
so it won't earn attention" — but it always resolves the challenge into the tighter
version: the narrower topic set, the missing anchor, the voice that should own it, the
proof that would make it un-fakeable, the repackaging left on the table.

## What good looks like

- **One narrative, everything ladders to it.** Scattered content with no throughline is
  the default failure. Anchors ladder to signature topics; topics ladder to the
  brand narrative / point of view.
- **Big, bold, few topics.** Signature topics are universal and limited — usually
  3–4 a year — and align *all* outbound comms (PR, social, email, sales), not just the
  blog. More than four and none get owned.
- **Anchors are destinations; SEO is one way to build one, not the definition.** An
  anchor is the definitive hub for a topic — it can be a report, a recurring show, a
  tool, a course, a definitive essay, *or* a pillar page. Regular pieces feed and
  collate up to it (Intercom's Jobs-to-be-Done books are the canonical shape).
- **Right content, right stage, right segment, right medium.** A piece is defined as
  much by where in the funnel and for whom and on what surface as by its topic.
- **The narrative is carried by people.** Design the voice/creator architecture on
  purpose — founder, execs, employees, external creators, the brand account — many
  authentic voices, one throughline. Don't flatten them into corporate mush or let
  them fragment into noise.
- **Distribution is the first constraint, not the last step.** Decide where attention
  is and design for the surface before creating. The website is a system of record and
  a conversion surface, not the destination.
- **Repackaging is the leverage — with a human core.** One insight becomes an essay, a
  carousel, a thread, a talk, a newsletter, clips — deliberately. AI lowers the cost of
  *genuine* per-platform adaptation; it does not license copy-paste slop.
- **Every piece is un-fakeable and for something.** Each carries POV, proof or
  personality a model can't generate, *and* the OKR or storyline it supports. Content
  with no job, or that a model could have written, is cut.
- **Owners and cadence, or it's fiction.** Name who makes each format, in which voice,
  how often. "Nothing goes dark" beats "one channel pops."

## Working mode

Confirm three things up front, because they change everything downstream.

- **Facilitated or drafted?** *Facilitated*: walk the founder/team through the model
  live — best when the narrative and convictions live in their heads. *Drafted*:
  generate a v1 from existing positioning, POV, brand and content materials for them to
  react to — usually right when working from a client's assets. Either way the output
  is a "v1 to run", not scripture.
- **Client or own brand?** For a client, the deliverable is client-ready and hands off
  to their team/tools. For the user's own brand, it wires into the existing content
  operating system rather than standing alone.
- **What upstream work exists?** Positioning, point of view, storylines, brand
  foundations and any channel map are *inputs* — read them first. If missing, this
  skill still runs (see *Working with incomplete information*) but flags the gap rather
  than quietly inventing a narrative.

## Where this sits

```
[Positioning]   [Point of View]              [CONTENT MODEL]               [The content engine / content-OS]
      \          - POV / market belief        (this skill)                   editorial, social, video,
       \         - Storylines ───────────→   1 Foundation (the floor)        email, events, distribution
        → feed in as ────────────────────→    2 Voice & the un-fakeable core
           strategic input                    3 Distribution & repackaging   [Channel map]
      "what we are /   - Signature Topics   4 Operating system              - which channels, in what order
       what we believe" ─────────────────→                                     (consumed here, not decided here)

                                                                             [Messaging framework]
                                                                              - the words / claims
                                                                              (laddered to, not produced here)
```

**Inputs.** Point of view and storylines (from `point-of-view`) are the raw material —
signature topics are the hinge. Positioning and brand foundations set the narrative.
A channel map (from `channel-map`), if it exists, says which channels are Build-first;
this skill *consumes* that call, it does not re-prioritise channels.

**Outputs / consumers.** The architecture and roadmap become the brief for the content
engine (the user's content-OS, or a client's editorial/social/video/email teams).
Specific claims and language belong to `messaging-framework`; this skill ladders to
them but doesn't write them.

### Boundary notes (kept distinct so triggers don't fight)

- **vs. `point-of-view`** — POV owns the *belief* and the *storylines* and stops at the
  first cut of topics. Content-model *starts* from signature topics and builds the
  whole system around them. Need the belief? Route to POV first.
- **vs. `channel-map`** — channel-map decides *which routes to market* to back and in
  what order. Content-model decides *what content travels down them* and how it's
  reused. It reads the channel call as an input.
- **vs. `content-strategy` (imported)** — that skill is demand-*capture* flavoured
  (searchable vs shareable, keyword research by buyer stage, SEO pillars). Pull it in
  for the keyword universe behind an anchor. This skill is the demand-*creation*
  architecture and the operating model around it.
- **vs. `icp`** — `icp` decides *who to sell to* and grades fit. This skill captures
  *who to write for and what makes them react*, which is a wider population and a
  different set of facts. Both live on the same personas and neither owns the other:
  the audience profile is a section added to a persona, not a replacement for it. A
  content finding that changes the buying picture goes back to `icp` rather than being
  written into the ICP from here.
- **vs. `messaging-framework`** — messaging owns the claims and proof language; this
  skill owns the structure they travel in.
- **vs. `campaign-brief` / `release-gtm`** — those are single-campaign or single-launch;
  this is the standing model they plug into.

## Architecture

Four phases, each with a reference file. **Determine the phase before loading a
reference — don't load all four at once.** For an end-to-end build, run 1 → 2 → 3 → 4.
Phases 2 and 3 are the overlay and only bite once the floor (Phase 1) is in place.

### Phase 1 — Foundation (the floor)
**When:** the user needs the *structure* — how the narrative becomes a navigable
hierarchy of content, mapped to the funnel and the segments.
**Produces:** the content pyramid (narrative → signature topics → anchors → regular
pieces → fragments, + the hygiene layer) and the funnel × segment × medium mapping.
This is the explicit anti-"random acts of marketing" gate.
**Reference:** `references/foundation.md`

**Also in Phase 1: the content audience profile.** The funnel × segment map says *who*;
it doesn't say what makes them react. Capture that separately — it is not the ICP, and
the audience for content is usually wider than the buying group. Do it when the client
has never written down who actually reads them, or when good content keeps failing to
travel. **Reference:** `references/audience-profile.md`

### Phase 2 — Voice & the un-fakeable core (the overlay that makes it 2026)
**When:** the architecture exists and the user needs to decide *who voices it* and
*what makes it un-generatable*.
**Produces:** the voice/creator architecture (the roster, each voice's lane, register
and surface, threaded to one narrative) and the moat gate (does each anchor/piece carry
POV, proof or personality a model can't fake?).
**Reference:** `references/voice-and-moat.md`

### Phase 3 — Distribution & repackaging (how it travels)
**When:** the architecture and voices exist and the user needs to decide how each piece
reaches an audience and earns its keep across surfaces.
**Produces:** the distribution model (channel mesh, distribution-first, native per
platform, consuming the channel map) and the repackaging / atomisation graph (one idea
→ pieces → fragments), plus the funnel × format × channel × owner map.
**Reference:** `references/distribution-and-repackaging.md`

### Phase 4 — Operating system (run it)
**When:** the model exists and the user needs to *run* it.
**Produces:** the content roadmap (the field-level template), cadence per format, the
production workflow (brief → produce → review → publish → distribute → repackage →
measure → feed back), the owner/RACI model including the human voices, and the
measurement model tying every piece to an OKR or storyline.
**Reference:** `references/operating-system.md`

## Routing logic

1. "Structure our content / signature topics / pillars / hierarchy / funnel
   mapping" → Phase 1.
1b. "Who actually reads us / who's our content audience / why doesn't this land / what
   makes them react / our posts are fine and nobody shares them" → the audience profile
   in Phase 1 (`references/audience-profile.md`). If they ask for the ICP or who to
   *sell* to, that's `icp` — different question, different profile.
2. "Who should voice this / founder-led / employee / creator content / what makes it
   un-fakeable in an AI world" → Phase 2.
3. "How do we distribute / go native per platform / repurpose / atomise" → Phase 3.
4. "Build the roadmap / cadence / who owns what / how do we measure it" → Phase 4.
5. "Build our whole content model / content system / content engine" → 1 → 2 → 3 → 4.
6. User has POV/positioning and asks broadly about content strategy → start Phase 1,
   confirm narrative and topics, then proceed.
7. Concept/definition question → answer from Core concepts + the relevant reference.

## Core concepts (always in context)

### The content pyramid (the floor)
Five levels plus a hygiene layer, each laddering to the one above.

```
            Brand narrative / Point of view         ← why we exist / what we believe (input)
                        │
            Signature Topics (3–4 / year)        ← big, bold, universal; align ALL comms
                        │
            Content Anchors (hubs)                   ← definitive destination per topic
                        │                              (report, show, tool, course, essay, OR pillar page)
            Regular pieces (the spider-web)          ← evergreen; feed & collate up to anchors
                        │
            Fragments (the atoms)                    ← short-form clips, quotes, carousels, hooks
                        │
            + ongoing help / hygiene content         ← product & support surface, always-on
```

If a proposed piece can't be placed on this pyramid *and* mapped to a funnel stage and
segment, it doesn't belong in the model yet. That placement test is the gate against
random acts of marketing.

### Content as a model, not a calendar (the four tests)
For any piece or anchor, a model (not a calendar) can answer:
- **Purpose** — which signature topic / storyline / OKR does it advance?
- **Position** — which funnel stage, which segment, which channel/medium?
- **Provenance** — who voices it, and what un-fakeable core (POV / proof / personality)
  does it carry that a model couldn't generate?
- **Propagation** — what does it repackage into, and which anchor does it collate up to?

### The floor: funnel × segment × medium
A piece is defined by *where*, *for whom* and *on what surface* as much as by *what*.
Default mapping (adapt per business, and treat as a lens, not a linear conveyor — the
real journey is non-linear and dark-social): **Awareness** → POV essay, podcast,
social-native, talk; **Consideration** → how-to, comparison, framework, webinar;
**User/Activation** → product-discovery content, docs-as-content, tutorials;
**Buyer/Decision** → case study, ROI content, sales enablement. Anchors usually live
mid-funnel; the spider-web spans the whole journey. Cross every stage with the segment
(ICP/persona) so a piece is aimed, not sprayed.

### The overlay 1: voice / creator architecture
The narrative is no longer held by the logo; audiences trust the face over the brand.
Design a roster on purpose — founder/CEO, execs, employees/SMEs, external creators, the
brand account — where each voice owns a lane (some signature topics), posts in its
own authentic register on its native surface, and threads back to the one narrative.
The coherence mechanism is shared narrative + topic ownership, *not* enforced uniform
tone. Manage key-person risk (a founder who leaves or won't post) with multiple voices
and brand-owned formats. Detail in `references/voice-and-moat.md`.

### The overlay 2: the un-fakeable core (the moat gate)
When a model can generate infinite competent content, the scarce inputs are the only
real product: **point of view, proof/receipts (lived experience, data, named
examples), personality, and taste.** The gate: for each anchor and flagship piece,
which of these does it carry? If none, a model could have written it and it won't earn
attention — upgrade it (inject POV/proof/personality) or cut it. This is the AI-age
version of "no content from the armchair."

### The overlay 3: distribution-first and the mesh
Channels have proliferated into a mesh, not a hub-and-spoke. Discovery happens in-feed,
through people, and inside AI answer engines — as well as via search. So decide the
surface before creating, and treat search / AEO-GEO / native social / creator reach as
*parallel* plays, each with a role — not either/or, and not one gutting the others. The
SEO pillar page still captures demand where buyers search; it is simply no longer the
sole organising logic. Detail in `references/distribution-and-repackaging.md`.

### The one-narrative rule
Aim for ~90% of non-hygiene content to ladder to a signature topic this year, and
for the *combination* of topics to be ownable by this brand and no competitor. If
everything is "on brand" but nothing is *the* brand, the topics are too broad — or the
voices have drifted.

## The content roadmap (Phase 4 template)

The roadmap is the model's backlog. Each row is one planned piece; the fields make the
four tests concrete. Group into blocks (full field guide, value examples and
prioritisation logic in `references/operating-system.md`):

**Brief** — Content type · Signature topic + anchor it feeds · Primary segment /
ICP · Funnel stage · Primary channel(s) · Gated / CTA / lead magnet? · Primary creative

**Voice & production** — Human voice / owner (from the roster) · Author / producer ·
Format · Other resources · Reviewers

**Prioritisation & scope** — Planned quarter or month · Size / effort · Potential
impact · Quarterly OKR it supports · Storyline or pillar it supports

**Propagation & proof** — Fragment / atomisation plan (what it repackages into) ·
Un-fakeable core (POV / proof / personality it carries) · Measurement (what it's for +
leading/lagging signal)

Prioritise by impact × effort against the OKRs, not by whoever shouts loudest. A
high-effort anchor earns its slot by the size of the spider-web it will seed; balance
the backlog across topics, funnel stages and voices so nothing goes dark.

## Output formats

### Phase 1 — Foundation
- The narrative core in 1–2 lines (what everything ladders to)
- 3–4 signature topics, each with its one substantive claim and its "why us"
- Per topic: the anchor(s) — the destination hub(s), with their form named (report /
  show / tool / essay / pillar) — and a starter set of regular-piece and fragment angles
- The funnel × segment × medium map, and the one-narrative check

### Phase 2 — Voice & the un-fakeable core
- The voice/creator roster: each voice's lane (topics owned), native surface, register,
  and throughline link; the coherence + key-person-risk notes
- The moat-gate pass over the anchors/flagships: what un-fakeable core each carries, and
  what to upgrade or cut

### Phase 3 — Distribution & repackaging
- The distribution model: channel mesh, which calls are inherited from the channel map,
  the role of each surface (search / AEO / social / creator)
- The repackaging graph for the flagship content types
- The funnel × format × channel × owner map (a table)

### Phase 4 — Operating system
- The populated roadmap (fields above), at least one quarter deep
- Cadence per format + the "nothing goes dark" minimum
- Production workflow and the owner/RACI model (including the human voices)
- Measurement: per content type, what it's for and the leading/lagging signal, plus
  audience-relationship metrics — not just traffic
- Handoff note: what goes to the content engine / client team vs `messaging-framework`

## Working with incomplete information

Content models are often commissioned before positioning and POV are finished.

1. **Flag what's absent** — name the missing narrative/POV/positioning/channel input.
2. **Give hypothesis-quality answers** — frame the narrative core and topics as a
   "working spine to validate", not fact.
3. **Recommend the right room** — the signature-topics call needs founders + product
   marketing + a customer-facing voice; the voice architecture needs the actual humans
   who'll post. Name who should be present.
4. **Surface tension, don't average it** — if two candidate narratives pull apart, name
   the fork. A model laddering to a mushy narrative compounds the mush.

## Client engagement vs your own brand

Written brand-agnostic and self-contained, so it runs for any client or for the user's
own brand.

- **In a client engagement:** read the engagement's context (CONTEXT.md, positioning,
  POV, channel map) before Phase 1. Treat the strategic spine as *propose, don't
  overwrite* — the roadmap and working docs accrete freely; the narrative, topic set and
  voice roster are ratified with the client, not assumed. Hand off to the sibling skills
  named in *Where this sits* rather than duplicating them.
- **For the user's own brand:** the model wires into the existing content operating
  system (its strategy, pipeline and distribution stages) rather than producing a
  standalone artefact. Respect its anti-themes and cadence.

## Method lineage

The idea of a small set of standing topics a brand sets out to own, laddering down
from one narrative, draws on MKT1 (Emily Kramer)'s writing on topics of authority and
content strategy. The pyramid, the four phases and the operating model here are our own
structure and wording.
