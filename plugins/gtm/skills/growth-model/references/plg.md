# Product-led growth (and product-led sales)

**What it is.** The product is the primary acquisition, activation and expansion
engine: users self-serve to value before (or instead of) talking to a human. PLG
proper is self-serve end to end; **product-led sales (PLS)** uses product usage as
the signal that triggers a sales motion (the honest middle ground for most B2B).

**When it fits.** Fast time-to-value a single user can reach alone; low/no setup;
a job a user can adopt without procurement; a natural multi-user or multi-account
surface that creates a loop (collaboration, sharing, content). Pricing that supports
a self-serve entry (free tier / cheap first seat) without starving the business.

**When it doesn't.** Long time-to-value or heavy onboarding; a buyer who is not the
user (security, procurement, exec sign-off dominate); high ACV with few accounts
where sales economics beat self-serve; a product that only shows value once
configured by IT. Forcing PLG here produces signups that never activate and a "free
users won't convert" problem that is really a fit problem.

**Engine shape.**
- Engine: self-serve adoption → activation → expansion (seats/usage) → (often) a
  collaboration or content loop that pulls new users.
- Fuel: new users reaching value; the product surface that invites others.
- Lubricants: onboarding, activation to the aha, in-product upgrade prompts, usage
  telemetry that feeds PLS.
- Boosts: launches, directory features. Reinvestment: users bring users; usage data
  improves the product.

**Fit tests that bite.** Product-motion fit (can a user truly self-serve to value?);
ICP fit (is the user the buyer, or does a human have to close?); channel-model fit
(does the free→paid economics work, or is the free tier just cost?).

**Decay signals.** Activation rate sliding; free→paid conversion falling; expansion
flattening; rising share of signups that never reach value; sales quietly required to
close the deals that "should" self-serve (a sign the real motion is PLS or sales-led).

**Lineage.** OpenView (PLG canon, the PLG benchmarks); Reforge (product-channel fit);
Sean Ellis / Rahul Vohra (must-have signal before scaling self-serve).
