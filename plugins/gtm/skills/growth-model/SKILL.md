# Growth model

This skill answers the question upstream of channel strategy:

> How should this business grow, as a system?

The goal is a clear growth thesis: what engine can compound, what fuels it, what
improves its efficiency, what is only a temporary boost, what motion fits the
business, and what must be proven next. It sits between ICP and execution:

`icp` -> `growth-model` -> `growth-bets` / `channel-map` -> execution skills

## Posture

Constructive challenge is the value. The skill can say a plan is structurally
misaligned, but it must never stop there.

Every challenge resolves into one of:

- the better model
- the narrower version that could work
- the missing condition that would make it viable
- the cheapest credible proof step

The tone is optimistic, practical and action-oriented: "this version fights the
model; here is the version with a better chance of working."

## What good looks like

- **Model before motion before channel.** Do not start with "try SEO" or "run
  LinkedIn". First decide how growth can compound.
- **Loops beat funnels, but funnels still diagnose leaks.** AARRR is useful for
  finding broken stages; it is not enough to explain how growth reinvests into more
  growth.
- **Growth is constrained by fit, not enthusiasm.** Check product-channel fit,
  channel-model fit, ICP fit, founder/team fit, and stage fit.
- **Most early growth problems are sequencing problems.** Teams optimise, hire,
  diversify, or automate before the model has earned it.
- **A channel is not a strategy.** If there is no reinvestment mechanism, it is
  probably a boost, tactic, or capacity drain.
- **Recommend the model the evidence supports, not the one in fashion.** PLG is not
  a religion; for many B2B products founder-led, sales-led or sales-assisted is the
  honest model. This is a sober, evidence-led call, not a contrarian pose — go where
  the pull and the economics actually point.

## The model & motion library (references/)

Deep profiles of each growth model and motion live in `references/`, so this file
stays the selector and the deep detail loads only when it's relevant. Read the
relevant profile(s) when mapping the model (step 3) and choosing the motion
(step 5). Each profile carries: what it is, when it fits, when it does not, its
engine shape, the fit tests that bite, and its characteristic decay signals.

Seed library (expandable — add a file as the repertoire grows):

- `references/loops.md` — growth loops vs funnels, the racecar logic
- `references/plg.md` — product-led growth and product-led sales
- `references/sales-led.md` — sales-led and sales-assisted inbound
- `references/founder-led.md` — founder-led sales / founder-led growth
- `references/community-led.md` — community and ecosystem pull
- `references/partner-led.md` — partner / channel / white-label motions
- `references/retention.md` — retention, churn and expansion. Not a motion profile: the
  diagnostic for when the base leaks. Read it whenever churn or retention is the presenting
  problem, and whenever the model is subscription or usage-based (there retention is the
  engine, not a lubricant, and the ceiling it sets is usually the finding).

If the right model is not yet in the library, reason from first principles, name
that the profile is missing, and write a new reference file so the next run inherits
it.

## Inputs to read

Use the best available evidence. Do not invent it.

- Existing ICP, positioning, messaging, strategy or GTM docs.
- Product context: what the product does, time-to-value, onboarding, usage patterns,
  collaboration surfaces, data/content generated by users.
- Commercial context: ACV, pricing model, sales cycle, buyer/user split, expansion
  path, payback constraints.
- Traction evidence: retention, activation, win/loss, source mix, pipeline, revenue,
  organic pull, referrals, founder-led sales notes.
- Capacity: who can actually run growth work, how much time they have, and what they
  are good at.
- Current channel or campaign plan, if one exists.

If evidence is thin, say so. Thin evidence caps confidence, not usefulness.

## Workflow

### 1. Lead with the growth read

Open with a short answer, max 4 lines:

- current model, if visible
- recommended model or motion
- biggest constraint
- next proof step

Do this before detail. The user should know the call immediately.

### 2. Establish stage and pull

Decide whether the business is:

- pre-PMF / search
- early PMF / founder-led validation
- scaling a proven motion
- adding a second engine
- reviving stalled growth

Then grade the pull signal:

- must-have / "very disappointed" evidence
- retention and repeat usage
- activation to the core value
- organic inbound, referrals or word-of-mouth
- sales cycle quality and win-rate signal
- expansion or repeat purchase

If pull is weak, growth work becomes learning work. The recommendation should shift
toward sharper ICP, activation, retention, proof, or founder-led learning before
scaling channels.

### 3. Map the growth model

Use a practical version of the Reforge racecar / loop logic. Read
`references/loops.md` for the depth.

| Component | Question |
|---|---|
| Engine | What repeatable mechanism can drive most growth? |
| Fuel | What input does the engine consume: capital, content, users, sales capacity, data, proof, community, inventory? |
| Lubricants | What makes the engine more efficient: activation, conversion, retention, onboarding, pricing, proof, sales enablement? |
| Leak | What proportion of each cycle's output is lost before it can be reinvested? |
| Boosts | What can spike growth but not compound: PR, launch, events, one-off campaigns, founder stunts? |
| Reinvestment | How does output from one cycle feed the next? |

Name whether the model is actually a loop, a funnel, a services-led sales motion, or
a set of disconnected tactics. Do not force a loop where one does not exist.

If the leak is large — or if the business is subscription or usage-based, where the base
*is* the engine — stop and read `references/retention.md` before going further. A model
that adds customers to a leaking base has a ceiling, and naming that ceiling is usually
worth more than any channel recommendation downstream of it.

### 4. Run the fit tests

Use these tests to find structural mismatches:

| Fit test | What to check |
|---|---|
| ICP fit | Is the model built around buyers/users who can feel the pain and act? |
| Product-channel fit | Does the product naturally fit the rules of the likely channel? |
| Channel-model fit | Does ACV/pricing/payback support the channel cost and friction? |
| Product-motion fit | Does the product support the motion: self-serve, sales-assisted, enterprise, founder-led, partner-led? |
| Team/founder fit | Can the actual people run this with energy and competence? |
| Stage fit | Is the business trying to scale, optimise, diversify or hire too early? |
| Data fit | Can the team see the inputs and outcomes needed to learn? |

When a fit fails, write the constructive version: what would need to change.

### 5. Decide the motion inside the model

Motion is a section of this skill, not a separate skill for now. Read the relevant
`references/` profile for the chosen motion's fit conditions.

Possible motions: founder-led sales / founder-led growth, sales-led, sales-assisted
inbound, product-led growth, product-led sales, content-led, partner / ecosystem-led,
community-led, hybrid.

State the recommended motion now, and the likely next motion later. If the answer is
hybrid, name the sequence. "Hybrid" is not permission to do everything at once.

### 6. Separate engines, boosts, and distractions

Classify current or proposed activities:

- **Engine:** can repeat and reinvest into more growth.
- **Lubricant:** improves conversion, activation, retention, sales efficiency or
  payback.
- **Boost:** useful spike, but temporary.
- **Learning bet:** proves a model assumption.
- **Distraction:** not aligned to the model, stage, ICP or capacity.

This is where the skill earns its judgement. Be clear, but make the next action
constructive.

### 7. Define the next proof

Close the diagnostic with the cheapest credible proof steps. For each, name:
assumption tested, action, evidence needed, decision it unlocks, handoff skill (if
any). This is usually where `growth-bets` starts.

### 8. Define the engine-health watch

A growth model is a living thesis, not a one-off verdict — engines decay (loops
saturate, channels get more expensive, a once-strong motion stops compounding). So
before closing, define the watch that tells you whether the engine is still earning
its place. Name:

- **Compounding signals** (it's still the engine): the loop is reinvesting (output
  feeds more input), organic / word-of-mouth share trending up, payback holding or
  shortening.
- **Decay signals**: CAC creeping, activation or conversion sliding, organic
  plateauing, and the tell — growth increasingly propped by *boosts* (PR, paid
  spikes) rather than the loop, or the channel saturating.
- **Re-model trigger**: the threshold at which to re-run this skill — e.g. the
  engine's share of new growth drops below X, payback degrades past Y, or the team is
  leaning on boosts to hit the number. At that point the engine may have decayed into
  a boost, or it is time for a second engine.

Define it even when the signals are not yet measurable, and flag what the team must
instrument to see them. (Lineage: Andrew Chen, channel decay.)

## Handoffs

- Use `icp` when the target buyer/user is too vague to model growth honestly.
- Use `growth-bets` when the model is clear enough to turn into a ranked portfolio of
  near-term bets.
- Use `channel-map` when route-to-market detail and channel prioritisation are the
  next layer.
- Use metrics / measurement work when the model depends on signals the team cannot
  currently see.
- Use PMM / launch skills when the growth model is sound but the launch narrative,
  announcement, or product marketing surface is the work.
- Use org-design / hiring work when the constraint is human capacity or role design.

## Quality rules

1. Open with the call, not the framework.
2. Mark evidence quality. Do not make thin evidence sound strong.
3. Do not produce a generic growth lecture.
4. Do not recommend more than one primary engine unless there is evidence the team
   can run and sequence more than one.
5. Name boosts as boosts. They can be useful, but they are not the engine.
6. Treat motion as stage-specific. The right motion now may not be the right motion
   later.
7. Always end with a constructive proof path and an engine-health watch.
8. Keep the boundary with `channel-map`: this skill decides the model and motion;
   `channel-map` prioritises routes to market.
