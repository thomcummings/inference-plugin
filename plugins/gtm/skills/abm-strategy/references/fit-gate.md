# Fit gate — should this client run ABM?

A **lightweight qualification**, not a motion assessment. The whole-business
"how should this company grow" question belongs to `growth-model`. This gate only
catches obvious misuse of an account-based motion. Keep it short; a paragraph of
reasoning and a verdict is enough.

## The read
Pass if the economics and conditions clearly support treating a finite set of
accounts as the unit of work. Fail (→ hand to `growth-model`) if they don't. When
it's genuinely borderline, say so and name the one condition that would settle it.

## Qualification questions

**Deal economics.** Is the ACV / deal size high enough to justify per-account
cost? Account-based effort is expensive per account; it earns its keep when
deals are large and finite in number. As a rough industry prior, account-based
motions dominate above ~$25k ACV; below ~$5k / sub-$1M ARR, self-serve / PLG
usually wins on economics. Treat these as priors to pressure-test against the
client's real numbers, not hard rules.

**Finite, nameable market.** Can you actually name the accounts? ABM needs a
bounded target set (tens to low thousands), not an unbounded long tail. If the
best move is to reach an enormous undifferentiated market, that's demand
gen / PLG, not ABM.

**Buying-group complexity.** Are these committee purchases with multiple
stakeholders and a real evaluation? Account-based orchestration pays off when
several people must be engaged to win. A single-signature, single-persona
purchase rarely needs it.

**Sales capacity & willingness.** Is there sales resource to work these accounts,
and will sales and marketing actually operate *jointly* against a shared account
plan? Marketing-only ABM is the most common way the motion fails.

**Data / signal floor (sizing, not disqualifying).** How much can they see —
CRM, usage data, closed-won history, intent tooling? This doesn't decide fit; it
decides how sophisticated the programme can be (evidence-thin vs evidence-rich)
and how much of the systems/agentic layer is reachable.

## Disqualifiers — the documented failure modes
If the plan is really one of these, it isn't ABM; name it plainly.

- **"Display ads to a CSV."** Uploading a list and running ads/emails at it is
  targeted demand gen, not ABM. ABM is coordinated, account-level, buying-group-
  aware orchestration with sales.
- **"One playbook for all."** Treating every account identically instead of
  tiering by value/complexity wastes the expensive motion on accounts that don't
  merit it and under-serves the ones that do.
- **"Air cover for SDRs."** Positioning ABM as just more top-of-funnel noise for
  the SDR team misses its actual value: strategic prioritisation and joint
  account plans.

## Output of the gate
A one-paragraph verdict: pass / fail / borderline, the load-bearing reason, and
either the go-ahead into design or a clean handoff to `growth-model`.

When the gate passes but leadership still has to endorse the change, follow it with
the fuller readiness test and the leadership ask in `business-case.md`.
