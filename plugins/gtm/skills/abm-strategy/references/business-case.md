# Business case: should this company move to ABM, and how to ask for it

## Readiness test

ABM fits when most of these are true. Score each one as yes, partly or no, and show the result to leadership.

| Condition | Why it matters | Evidence to collect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The addressable market can be named | You can write the list, so it isn't infinite | A market map or segment list; rough count of fitting companies, often in the low hundreds |
| Deal size justifies high-touch effort | Bespoke work per account has to pay back | Average contract value of the last ten closed deals |
| Buying committees are visible | ABM works the committee, not one contact | Number of distinct people involved in recent deals |
| Sales-led traction exists | There's a motion to accelerate | Enterprise contracts signed in the last two quarters |
| Marketing's contribution is unclear | That's the problem ABM measurement solves | Share of pipeline with a marketing touch you can show |
| The headline metric is mostly self-serve volume | Volume metrics can't judge enterprise contribution | Split headline "deals" or "leads" into self-serve vs. enterprise |
| AI and automation are available to the team | Personalized activity across hundreds of accounts is now affordable | Existing agent or automation capability |

Three or more "no" answers: run a lighter target-account overlay on the existing motion instead of a full migration.

## Diagnosing the volume trap

The most persuasive slide is usually this one: "our headline number measures product-led volume, not enterprise pipeline". Build it like this:
1. Take the metric leadership watches (deals created, MQLs, sign-ups).
2. Split it by origin: self-serve, sales-created, partner, bulk import.
3. Show the enterprise share. It's typically a small single-digit percentage.
4. Put it next to the enterprise wins in the same period. They often rose while volume fell.

The conclusion: the current yardstick can't show marketing's role in enterprise revenue, so marketing spend can't be tied to revenue. Name that as the top risk.

## Why now (typical arguments)
- The market is defined and finite, not infinite.
- Contract values justify the cost of high-touch acquisition.
- Buyers now arrive as committees (technical, business, security, procurement) rather than one decision-maker.
- AI makes personalized activity across 200–300 accounts operationally possible for a small team.

Treat "200–300 accounts" as an order of magnitude, not a target: size the list to the team's capacity (`tiering-and-selection.md`).

## The leadership or board ask

Use one sentence, one change of yardstick, and one bounded experiment:

> Endorse ABM as our GTM operating model, with signal-based target-account KPIs replacing volume as marketing's yardstick. For awareness: a bounded paid test aimed at the target list this quarter.

Supporting points:
- What changes: a named, tiered list; one team; stage movement as the KPI.
- What doesn't: the CRM stays the system of record, and self-serve continues as the one-to-many layer.
- First 90 days: list agreed, stages locked, weekly rhythm running, command center v1, first stage-movement goals.
- How you'll know: target accounts progressing a stage per quarter, by tier.

## Inverting the model (explainer for the team)

| Traditional B2B | ABM |
| --- | --- |
| Go wide, collect leads, qualify, convert | Pick the accounts first, then engage them precisely and expand after signature |
| Marketing hands leads to sales | One team works each account |
| Success = volume (traffic, leads, sign-ups) | Success = named accounts moving stage |
| Content for a generic persona | Content for a segment × persona × stage |
| One contact per deal | A mapped buying committee |

## Common ABM failure modes (name them up front)

Full list in `failure-modes.md`. The five to show leadership:
- The target list stops being updated.
- Personalization theater: custom-looking assets that nobody in the buying group sees.
- Siloed teams: marketing runs "ABM campaigns" that sales doesn't know about.
- Vanity metrics creep back in (impressions, likes).
- Too many Tier 1 accounts, so nothing gets real one-to-one effort.
