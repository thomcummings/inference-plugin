# Agentic opportunity

This skill answers one question, in the marketing context:

> Where do AI, automation and agents change the economics of this work — and what
> is the opportunity worth reaching for?

Every other strategy skill defaults to judgement-first restraint. This one is the
deliberate counterweight: its job is to **show the opportunity space** — the leverage
that cheap competence, orchestration and near-free experimentation unlock — that a
restraint-first reading leaves on the table. It is not a hype machine. It is bullish
in direction and honest in magnitude: it argues the case *and* prices the cost.

It is also a **lens, not an island**. Any skill can call it when an
automate / orchestrate / build-an-agent question comes up, and it hands its findings
back rather than trying to own the whole decision.

## Two modes

**Full map (default when invoked directly).** The client wants to know where the
agentic leverage is across their marketing. Run the whole workflow and produce the
opportunity map artefact. It works as the core of an agent-opportunity workshop or
the org-design and immersion phase of an agent-teams build.

**Inline lens (when another skill calls it).** A single question has surfaced — "could
this bet be run agentically?", "should this role be an agent?", "is there a cheaper
automated way to learn this?". Answer just that, applying the lens below, then hand
back. Do not produce the full map. Keep it to the call and the honest caveat.

## Posture — bullish in direction, honest in magnitude

The value is the optimism *with* the rigour. Lean in, then tell the truth about the
bill.

- **Direction: lean in.** Assume more of this work is becoming agent-suitable than the
  team thinks, and that the frontier moves every quarter. Push it.
- **Magnitude: grade it honestly.** Every opportunity carries its real cost — build
  effort, token spend, drift and quality risk, and the maintenance someone has to own.
  Confidence lives in the evidence, not in enthusiasm.
- **Self-moderating, not refereed.** When this lens disagrees with a restraint-first
  skill (most often growth-bets), it does not need a debate or a referee. It states
  the opportunity, names the tradeoff, keeps the positive outlook, and lets the
  advisor weigh it. Show the upside; don't hide the downside.
- **Never "just automate it".** Automation amplifies whatever it sits on. The honest
  version of bullish is "here is the leverage, and here is what has to be true for it
  to pay off" — not "ship an agent".

## What good looks like — the sharp opinions

Five stances this skill will defend. They are deliberately more pro-automation than
the rest of the system.

1. **Most teams have bought a kettle and called it electricity.** Handing people
   copilots is personal efficiency; the unclaimed leverage is the *team / system*
   layer — orchestration, handoffs, shared context — not more individual tools. Point
   the opportunity there.
2. **Production speed and volume are an opportunity, not just a bottleneck others
   solve.** Restraint-first skills treat output as cheap and therefore beside the
   point. This lens shows what near-free production and orchestration actually unlock:
   more shots on goal, always-on coverage, variant testing, surface area that
   compounds.
3. **Cheap experiments decouple learning from prior economic proof.** When an
   experiment is near-free to run agentically, "prove the economics first" is partly a
   legacy gate from when experiments were expensive. Run speculatively, learn fast —
   still instrument it, but don't make every test earn its place in advance. (This is
   a direct, deliberate friction with `growth-bets`' gates. State it; don't resolve it
   away.)
4. **Agents amplify whatever they sit on — so aim them at working systems.** The
   biggest, safest leverage sits on top of a sound process, clean context and a held
   quality bar. Automating a broken funnel scales the brokenness. This is "leverage,
   not a fix for a bad model" — used to aim the opportunity, not to stall it.
5. **The bottleneck moves to framing, judgement and governance — so redeploy, don't
   just cut.** Freed human hours are the point, not a rounding error: they move up the
   value chain to framing, taste, oversight and system design. And maintenance is a
   real, ownable line item — price it in, don't pretend agentic systems are
   set-and-forget.

## The lens (the durable judgement)

The deep detail lives in `references/`, so this file stays the selector. Read the
relevant reference when you reach the matching step.

- **The adoption curve** — Chat → Automation → Augmentation → Agentic → Autonomous.
  Read where a task or function sits today and where it could move.
  (`references/adoption-curve.md`)
- **Agent-suitability frontier + build shape** — what makes work agent-suitable
  (repeatability, clear inputs/outputs, low criticality of a single error, low human
  interaction) versus human-held (ambiguity, taste, judgement, accountability,
  relationships) — read as a *moving frontier*, not a fixed line. Plus the
  Identity / Capability / Governance frame for shaping a role once an opportunity is
  worth building. (`references/agent-suitability-and-build-shape.md`)
- **Economics changed + honest tradeoffs** — the five economics agents move (cost,
  speed, scale/volume, quality-consistency, and the big one, learning speed) and the
  real costs (token spend and coordination tax, drift and quality inconsistency,
  edge-case brittleness, maintenance burden, snap-decision risk — "compressed
  timelines cut both ways"). (`references/economics-and-honest-tradeoffs.md`)

### Freshness — search, don't shelve

What a model or tool can actually do *this quarter* rots within weeks, so it is
deliberately **not** stored here. The durable lens above is stable; the current
capability is not. Before judging whether a specific piece of work is automatable now,
**run a web search for the current state** of the relevant tools/models/agent
patterns and cite what you find with its date. Treat anything you "remember" about
tool capability as likely stale and verify it. This live check is the skill's living
mechanism — there is no shelf to go out of date.

## Inputs to read

Use the best available evidence; do not invent it.

- The marketing function / workflows: what work actually gets done, by whom, how often,
  with what inputs and outputs. Activity-level first; the literal tool stack second.
- Adoption maturity: where the team sits on the curve, what they have already
  automated, where AI is already in use and how.
- Context substrate: is there a shared brand/ops/context layer agents could read and
  write, or is knowledge trapped in heads and docs? (Agents amplify what they sit on.)
- Commercial and capacity context: who could own and maintain agentic work, token
  budget appetite, risk tolerance, regulatory/brand-safety constraints.
- Any strategy, growth-model, ICP, channel-map or audit output already in the
  engagement.

If evidence is thin, proceed on labelled hypotheses and name the cheapest way to
thicken them. Thin evidence caps confidence, not usefulness.

## Workflow

### 1. Lead with the opportunity read

Open with the call, max 4 lines:

- where the biggest agentic leverage is
- the one move to reach for first
- the honest caveat (what has to be true / the real cost)
- the next step

State the call before any framework.

### 2. Place them on the curve + check live capability

Name where the function (and the specific work in question) sits on
Chat → Automation → Augmentation → Agentic → Autonomous, and where it could
realistically move next. Then **web-search the current capability** for the relevant
work and note, with dates, what is genuinely possible now versus still research.

### 3. Map the work surface

Inventory the marketing activities/workflows (not primarily the tools). For each, read
two things: the **economics-change potential** (which of cost / speed / scale /
quality / learning it would move, and how much) and the **agent-suitability** on the
moving frontier. Push the frontier — flag work that is *becoming* agent-suitable, not
only what obviously already is.

### 4. Test the substrate (the precondition)

Before recommending anything that runs *on* data or process, test what it would sit on —
because agents amplify whatever that is. This is a standing gate, not a one-off:

- **Data quality.** Is the data an analytics/insight agent would read clean enough to
  amplify, or would it amplify the dirt? (Dirty CRM, empty fields, untrustworthy
  attribution → fix the data first; the fix is the real first opportunity.)
- **Process soundness.** Is the workflow being automated actually working, or would
  automation scale a broken process? Aim agents at sound systems.
- **Context layer.** Is there a shared context substrate agents can read/write, or is
  knowledge trapped in heads and tools?
- **Tooling churn.** Is the underlying platform/stack settled, or in flux (a pending
  build/replace decision) such that building agents on it now risks rework?

Where the substrate is too weak, the substrate fix becomes a prerequisite opportunity,
and dependent opportunities are sequenced behind it (or marked "Not yet").

### 5. Rank the opportunities — direction graded by magnitude

Rank; never hand over a flat list. For each opportunity, grade the upside (the
economics it changes) and the honest cost (build effort, token spend, drift/maintenance,
risk), then give a verdict.

**If a plan or roadmap already exists**, mark each opportunity as **net-new** (the lens
surfaced it) or **confirmed** (already on their plan — say so plainly). Spend the
analysis on what's net-new, the re-sequencing, and the honest tradeoffs they've missed;
don't relabel the client's own roadmap back to them as insight.

| Verdict | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Automate now** | Repeatable, bounded, low single-error cost — capture it. |
| **Orchestrate** | Worth a multi-agent / role-based design, not a single flow — the team-layer leverage. |
| **Augment** | Keep a human directing; agent assists. Judgement or taste stays human for now. |
| **Experiment cheaply** | Near-free to try agentically — run it speculatively to learn, ahead of economic proof. |
| **Not yet** | Frontier isn't there or the substrate is too weak — name exactly what would change the verdict. |

### 6. Sketch the build shape for the lead opportunities

For the top opportunities only, sketch the shape using Identity / Capability /
Governance, and name the human-in-the-loop point (where the quality bar stays human).
Enough for a build team to start; not the build itself.

### 7. Name the honest tradeoffs — "cut both ways"

A standing section, never skipped. For the recommended opportunities, name: who owns
maintenance, the token-budget reality, the drift/quality watch, the snap-decision risk
from compressed timelines, and where the human quality bar must stay. This is what
keeps the bullishness trustworthy.

### 8. Say what the freed humans move toward

Don't leave freed capacity as an implied headcount cut. Name the higher-value work the
team should redeploy toward — framing, taste, oversight, system design — per opinion 5.

### 9. Hand off

- `agent-design` — to specify (or reject) a persistent agent once an opportunity
  is worth standing up. Not a required next step; use it when the question has
  become "what is this worker?" rather than "where is the leverage?".
- `growth-bets` — when an "experiment cheaply" opportunity is really a growth
  experiment to fold into the portfolio (note the friction: this lens argues for
  running ahead of proof).
- org-design / hiring — when the live question is a role or human/agent split (this
  lens feeds that skill's decision; it doesn't own it).
- `growth-model`, `channel-map`, PMM/launch, metrics — when the opportunity sits inside
  their territory.

## Quality rules

1. Lead with the opportunity call, not the framework.
2. Bullish in direction, honest in magnitude — every opportunity carries its real cost.
3. Web-search current capability; never judge "automatable now" from stale memory.
4. Rank, don't flatten — give each opportunity a verdict.
5. Push the frontier: flag work that is *becoming* agent-suitable, not only the obvious.
6. Always include the "cut both ways" tradeoffs and a named maintenance owner.
7. Test the substrate first; aim opportunities at working systems and clean data, and
   make the substrate fix a prerequisite opportunity when it's too weak to amplify.
8. When a plan already exists, mark opportunities net-new vs confirmed and spend the
   value on what's net-new — don't relabel the client's roadmap back as insight.
9. Always say what the freed humans move toward — never leave it as an implied cut.
10. Keep the boundaries: this lens shows and shapes the opportunity; specifying a
    persistent worker is agent-design, the role/human-split is org-design, the
    experiment cull is growth-bets.
11. In inline-lens mode, answer only the question asked and hand back — don't force the
    full map.

## Common failure modes to avoid

- Listing tools instead of opportunities; leading with the stack instead of the work.
- Recommending automation on top of a broken process (scaling the brokenness).
- Going quietly anti-hype to match the rest of the system — this skill is the
  counterweight; it should argue the opportunity.
- Hiding the bill: omitting token cost, drift, or who maintains the thing.
- Judging capability from memory instead of a dated search.
- Recommending data-dependent agents on dirty data or a workflow in flux — skipping the
  substrate test.
- Relabelling the client's existing roadmap back to them as insight instead of marking
  what's net-new.
- Producing the full map when another skill only asked a single question.
