# Agent-suitability frontier + build shape

Two durable frames: what makes work agent-suitable (read as a *moving* line), and how
to shape a role once an opportunity is worth building.

## The suitability frontier

Hire an agent when the work is:

- **Repeatable** — same shape, same inputs, same expected outputs.
- **Boundable** — clear inputs and outputs; a one-paragraph job description is writable.
- **Low single-error cost** — a wrong output is cheap to catch and fix, not catastrophic.
- **Low human-interaction** — doesn't depend on relationships, community, or trust built
  in person.
- **Not creativity-critical** — craft isn't the core of the value (the most debatable
  test, and the one moving fastest — hold it loosely).

Keep human when the work needs:

- **Ambiguity-handling and judgement under context.**
- **Taste** — knowing what "good" and "right" look like, beyond "plausible".
- **Accountability** — someone has to own the outcome and the frame.
- **Relationships** — customers, community, stakeholders.
- **System design** — deciding how the whole thing fits together.

**Read it as a frontier, not a fixed line.** This skill's bullish job is to push it:
flag work that is *becoming* agent-suitable as the frontier moves, not only what's
obviously automatable today. Pair this judgement with a live capability search — the
frontier's current position is an empirical question, not a memory.

**The amplification caveat.** Agents amplify whatever they sit on. Suitable work on a
broken process or a missing context layer is *not* a good opportunity — it scales the
brokenness. Aim suitability at sound systems with a clean context substrate.

## Build shape — the three layers of a role

When an opportunity is worth building, shape it on these layers (from *Marketing
Rebuilt #2*). Enough to hand to a build; specifying a persistent worker is
`agent-design` when you want that step.

**Identity** — who the agent is. Role, scope, who it reports to, what it can delegate,
its character. The stable core; it survives a model swap underneath.

**Capability** — what it knows and can do. Skills, tools, the static business context it
loads each run, the memory it carries between runs. Most public agent talk lives only
here — it's a third of the picture.

**Governance** — what keeps it productive and safe. Targets, constraints it can't
violate, escalation paths at the edge of competence, routines that keep team rhythm,
and evals that tell you the work is *good*, not just plausible.

Always name the **human-in-the-loop point**: where the quality bar stays human, when
the agent escalates, what it can't do without sign-off. The quality bar is where the
system lives or dies — without a human holding it, agent teams drift toward plausible-
but-wrong.

## The "hire" framing

The most legible way in: treat it like hiring. You wouldn't post a job ad saying "good
at marketing"; you'd write a JD, agree ownership, define what success looks like. An
agent brief has the same shape — Role, Skills, Tool access, Cost/model, Boundaries.
Specific beats generic every time ("runs a JTBD analysis" beats "good at research").
