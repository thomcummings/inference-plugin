# Services and productised-consulting offer architecture

Do not copy SaaS tiering doctrine into services. For services, the equivalent of a
tier is a **responsibility contract** — how much the buyer still owns versus how much
the provider takes off them. The unit that scales is delivery depth, cadence, access
and scope control, not a feature list.

## The responsibility/capacity ladder

Translate "value metric" into the responsibility unit:

| Rung | What the buyer gets | Who owns the outcome | Typical shape |
|---|---|---|---|
| Self-serve asset | template, framework, cockpit | buyer | product / low-ticket |
| Diagnostic / map / audit | a decision-ready read | buyer acts on it | fixed-scope, fixed-fee |
| Bounded sprint | a defined build/implementation, time-boxed | shared | fixed scope, fixed window |
| Advisory access | judgement on demand, cadence, context | buyer executes | retainer / access |
| Managed implementation | provider does the work | provider | priced as delivery |
| Ongoing function / agent team | provider runs the operating layer | provider | premium retainer |

Each rung up transfers more responsibility and should command a step-change in price —
because the buyer is offloading more risk and work, not buying more hours.

## Package clarity test (services)

A services package is ready when the buyer can answer:

- What decision or outcome does this get me?
- What do I still have to do myself?
- Where does the engagement stop?
- What happens when I need more?

If the answer to "where does it stop?" is fuzzy, margin is at risk before a price is
even set.

## Margin guardrails for advisory / retainer

Protect the advisory margin — the value is diagnose, design, decide, direct. Do not let
produce / manage / operate / chase / maintain slide into a fixed fee unless the offer
is explicitly priced as implementation.

Cap the things that scale without bound:

- expert access / escalation → fair-use terms, response-time bands, not "unlimited"
- custom implementation → separate line, separate price, or explicitly excluded
- tool / inference cost → capped or passed through
- bespoke deliverables → a named, finite list
- onboarding effort → priced in, not absorbed

## Pricing shape by evidence level

- **No customers / hypothesis only** — sell the diagnostic first. It is the cheapest
  way to earn the right to the sprint, and it generates the evidence that prices the
  sprint honestly.
- **Some closed deals** — anchor the sprint against the avoided cost (bad hire, wrong
  build, wasted spend), not against hours.
- **Repeat buyers / renewals** — introduce the retained operating layer once the
  ongoing reason to stay is proven, not before.

## Anti-patterns

- Selling unlimited implementation inside a "retainer".
- Pricing a sprint by day rate × days, which invites scope creep and caps the upside.
- One offer trying to be diagnostic, sprint and retainer at once — collapse of the
  responsibility contract.
- Discounting the diagnostic to nothing, which signals it is worthless and weakens the
  ladder above it.
