# Operating model

## One team
ABM fails when marketing and sales work separately. Everyone needs to be able to answer, for any target account: where is it in the funnel, what's the next action, and who owns it.

## Roles

| Role | Responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| ABM owner | Owns the method, the list's integrity, stage definitions, the monthly recalibration and reporting |
| Account owner (one per account) | Accountable for stage movement and next steps. Supported by others. |
| Executive or founder | Senior-stakeholder relationships on Tier 1 |
| Sales or partnerships | Commercial progress and deal stages in the CRM |
| Solutions or forward-deployed engineer | Technical evaluation. Becomes the primary technical touchpoint for evaluating accounts. |
| Marketing | Plays and programs, content by segment × persona × stage, paid, events |
| DevRel or community (where present) | Practitioners at target accounts: finds them in sign-ups and communities, supports evaluation |
| Ops or agents | Signals, sync, reminders and reports. Proposes only. |

## Cadence

| Rhythm | Who | Output |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Daily (optional) | Account owners | Start and end the day on the command center. Optional morning rundown per person from an agent. |
| Weekly ABM meeting (60 min) | Sales, solutions engineering, marketing rep, founders as needed | Stage confirmations, blockers, and **the top 1–3 actions per function for the week**, each with an owner |
| Weekly GTM meeting | Wider GTM | ABM summary line plus funnel metrics; marketing actions agreed |
| Monthly ABM recalibration | ABM owner, sales lead, marketing lead | Re-tier, promote or retire accounts, adjust clusters and programs, review stage velocity |
| Quarterly | Leadership | Re-score ICP, set this quarter's wins, set marketing stage-movement goals, review scorecards |

How to start: rename the existing sales or account weekly to "ABM weekly" and add marketing. Don't add a new meeting.

## Weekly meeting agenda (60 min)
1. (5) What moved: stage changes since last week, auto-suggested and confirmed.
2. (10) This quarter's wins: each focus account's next step and blocker.
3. (20) Tier 1 walk-through, sorted by heat: hot accounts first, then those with overdue next steps.
4. (10) Signals worth acting on: new people from target accounts, pricing or docs reads, sign-ups.
5. (10) Marketing asks and offers: content, events and programs needed this week.
6. (5) Close: the top 1–3 actions per function. The ABM owner records them in the command center.

## Marketing's commitments under ABM
- Once the list is set, commit to **stage-movement goals**, for example "move N Tier 2 accounts from Unaware to Aware this quarter".
- Rethink content around target-account clusters rather than general demand.
- Join the weekly account review, either directly or through a named representative.
- Use paid to reach Unaware accounts at scale.
- Re-assess events and other programs against ABM goals.

## Internal comms
Share the ABM approach with the whole company early: why, the list (by tier, without sensitive notes), the stages, and how people can help (intros, expertise). People outside GTM often hold the warm intro that unlocks a stalled account.

## Hand-offs
- Product sign-ups from a target domain go to the account owner within a day.
- Accounts that cross into product-qualified usage (a PQL, using a definition sales has signed off) are flagged into the sales funnel.
- Owner changes are logged on the account.

## Habits that keep the rhythm alive
- **End every weekly meeting with the top 1–3 actions per function.** Without it,
  urgency stays invisible.
- **Run the review inside the meeting**, from the command center, rather than
  posting it to chat. Keep chat for personal items: tag digests and due reminders.
- **Engineers and solutions staff need their own view** of the accounts they
  support.
- **Iterate the command center from what's asked for in the meeting.** Feature
  requests from the weekly are the backlog.
