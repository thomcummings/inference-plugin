# Pressure-test

This skill answers one question: **should we actually do this, or are we about to
spend on a bet that won't pay?**

`growth-bets` says: "here are the best moves to make."
`root-cause` says: "here's why the thing that broke, broke."
`pressure-test` says: "you're about to commit to this one move. Let me try to kill it
before it costs you. If it survives, here's the smallest way to prove it first."

It is a gate, not a brainstorm and not a debate. It holds the skeptic's seat itself,
grounded in the client's own context, and resolves to a decision the user can act on
today.

## The objectivity dividend (why this isn't a room of voices)

The value here is one disciplined judgement, anchored in this client's reality, not a
chorus of synthetic personas arguing. So pressure-test does not stage a debate or
role-play a panel. It reasons as the advisor: reads what's known, names the strongest
case *and* the strongest case against, grades the evidence, and makes the call. The
friction comes from evidence and forcing questions, not from invented characters.

## Posture

Sceptical by default, constructive on exit. The job is to find the cheapest reason this
fails *before* the client pays to discover it. But never a bare "no". Every verdict
that isn't a clean proceed must resolve into one of: the narrower version that could
work, the missing condition to meet first, the cheaper proof to run, or the better-timed
later move. Flag the doubt once, hard, then commit to the most useful path forward.

Dial to zero on numbers, spend and bad news (dry mode). A go/no-go
on someone's money is not the place for flourish.

## When to use

- A specific, proposed move is on the table and a commitment is imminent.
- The user is excited and wants a gut-check, or uneasy and wants permission to stop.
- Real money, real time, or real opportunity cost is about to be spent.
- Before a launch, a channel test that needs budget, a rebrand, an agency retainer, a
  big content push.

## When NOT to use

- "What should we do next?" with no proposed move yet -> `growth-bets`.
- "Why did this campaign flop?" -> `root-cause`.
- "How should this whole business grow?" -> `growth-model`.
- The move is small, cheap and reversible. Pressure-testing a £200 reversible test is
  ceremony. Tell them to just run it and watch the signal.

## Inputs to read first (don't ask what you can look up)

Before interrogating the user, read the client's context store. Asking for what's
already on disk makes this a form, not an advisor.

- `evergreen/positioning.md` — is this move on-spine or off it?
- `evergreen/audience/personas.md` — who is it aimed at, and is that buyer real here?
- `active/priorities.md`, `active/projects/` — what's already in flight; does this
  compete for the same capacity?
- `memory/decisions/` — have we decided on this, or against it, before? Surface it.
- `memory/learnings/` — have we tried this or its cousin? What happened?
- `evergreen/operations/measurement.md` — can we even see the result if we run it?
- `evergreen/proof/proof.md`, `evergreen/channels/` — what evidence already exists for
  or against the mechanism?

Cite `canonical` only; treat `working` as hypothesis and say so; respect `updated:`
dates on numbers (context-store house rules). If a past decision already settled this,
treat it as a witness, not a verdict: name it, then re-derive from the evidence rather
than inheriting the conclusion.

## Workflow

### 1. Lead with the provisional call

Open in 3-4 lines, before any framework, with the lean from context alone:

- the provisional verdict (lean kill / lean shrink / lean proceed / can't-call-yet)
- the single biggest reason
- the one or two things that would change the call

This respects the user's time and surfaces the crux immediately. Everything after this
is earning or overturning that lean.

### 2. Run the pressure tests — only the load-bearing ones

Six tests. Do **not** march through all six. Skip any the context already answers, and
spend the interrogation on the one or two that actually decide it. Where you must ask
the user, ask tightly and offer a short menu of candidate answers with your lean
flagged, so they react rather than write essays (Slack-native concision). Push once past
the polished first answer; the real answer is usually the second one.

| # | Test | What it exposes | The red flag |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Pull reality** | Real evidence this works, vs "it seems like a good idea" | "It'll raise awareness", waitlist signups, "competitors do it", a gut feeling dressed as a trend |
| 2 | **Counterfactual** | What happens if we do nothing; what already does this job | "We just won't grow" with no mechanism; can't say what breaks without it |
| 3 | **Whose, exactly** | The specific buyer and what they currently do/say | A category, not a person ("SMBs", "marketers"); our words for the value, not theirs |
| 4 | **Narrowest wedge** | The smallest version that throws a real signal this week | "We need the whole funnel/site/campaign live before it works" |
| 5 | **Cost & reversibility** | True cost in money, attention and opportunity; can we stop cleanly | Cost counted in cash only, not attention; no kill switch; a one-way door framed as a try |
| 6 | **Model & spine fit** | Does it prove/advance the growth model and positioning, or drift off them; compound or one-off; and what axis it makes the buyer judge us on | Off-positioning "because it'll do numbers"; a boost mislabelled as an engine; a move that re-argues the decision on a competitor's strongest axis (e.g. price, where "do nothing" wins) |

Grade what you hear: validated, hypothesis, or assumption. The verdict's confidence
lives in that grading, not in adjectives. Thin evidence caps confidence, it doesn't stop
the call — proceed on labelled hypotheses and name the cheapest way to thicken them.

### 3. Apply the two hard gates

A move that fails either is not a "proceed", however exciting:

- **Spine gate.** Does it fit positioning and the growth model, or pull against them?
  Two ways to fail it, and the second is the one that's easy to miss: (a) it's simply
  off-positioning, or (b) **it reframes the buyer onto an axis where the status quo
  wins.** A move can be perfectly on-category and still quietly re-argue the decision
  on the competitor's strongest ground — e.g. a cost calculator that drags a
  simplicity-and-trust brand into a rent-vs-buy price fight the spreadsheet wins for
  "do nothing". Always ask: *what axis does this make the buyer judge us on, and do we
  win on that axis?* Off-spine moves that "get numbers" are how a brand blurs. If it
  fails either way, the honest output is shrink-to-the-on-spine-core or kill.
- **Visibility gate.** If we run this, can we actually see whether it worked? If the
  result is invisible (no tracking, no attribution, no baseline), the move isn't ready —
  it's *instrument-first*. Route to measurement before spend.

### 4. Settle the verdict

Drop the lean and weigh the surviving evidence honestly. One of:

- **Proceed (cheapest proof first).** It survives. Don't bless the full build — name the
  smallest test that resolves the riskiest assumption, with a pre-committed decision
  rule, then scale only on signal.
- **Shrink and test.** The full version is too big or too broad. Run the narrower wedge
  that produces a real signal this week.
- **Not yet — fix this first.** A foundation gap (no clear buyer, no positioning, no
  measurement) blocks an honest call. Name the gap and the skill that closes it. This is
  the route that "suggests skills to make a better decision".
- **Kill.** The bet doesn't survive and shrinking won't save it. Say so plainly, give the
  one reason, and point at where the energy should go instead.

### 5. If a foundation gap blocks the call, route to the skill that closes it

Pressure-test is allowed to stop and say "I can't responsibly green-light this until X is
known", then hand to the skill that produces X:

- buyer is vague -> `icp`
- positioning/spine unclear -> `positioning`
- the underlying growth logic is in doubt -> `growth-model`
- can't see the result -> measurement / instrumentation work
- it's really "what should we do instead" -> `growth-bets`

Name the gap, the skill, and what to bring back. Don't fake a verdict on missing
foundations.

## Output template

Keep it short. This is a decision record, not a deliverable. Adapt, keep the order.

```markdown
# Pressure-test: [the proposed move] — [date]

> Verdict: [Proceed (proof first) / Shrink and test / Not yet / Kill].
> One-line reason. The proof signal / the gap to close is [x].

## The move
What's being proposed, and the commitment it implies (spend, time, attention).

## What the context already says
2-4 lines from the store: prior decisions, what's in flight, evidence for/against.
Label canonical vs hypothesis.

## Pressure tests that mattered
Only the load-bearing ones. For each: what it exposed, evidence grade.
- Pull reality: ...
- [whichever others decided it]: ...

## Gates
- Spine fit: pass / fail — why
- Visibility: pass / instrument-first — why

## Verdict
The call, the single biggest reason, and confidence (with what it rests on).

## If proceeding: the cheapest proof
Smallest test, riskiest assumption it resolves, leading metric, pre-committed
decision rule, what scale looks like only on signal.

## If not: the better path
Shrink to [x] / close [gap] via [skill] / stop and put the energy into [y].

## Handoff
- growth-bets / channel-map / icp / positioning / measurement: ...
```

## Quality rules

1. Lead with the provisional verdict in the first lines, from context alone.
2. Read the context store before asking the user anything.
3. Never run all six tests by rote. Spend the questions on the cruxes.
4. State the strongest case *and* the strongest case against before calling it.
5. Grade evidence: validated / hypothesis / assumption. No confidence-by-adjective.
6. Apply both hard gates; a spine-fail or visibility-fail is never a clean proceed.
7. Every non-proceed verdict resolves into shrink, fix-first, or stop-and-redirect —
   never a bare no.
8. A proceed always names the cheapest proof and a pre-committed decision rule, not a
   green light to build the whole thing.
9. Flag the doubt once, hard; then commit. Don't re-litigate every turn.
10. Write the decision record to `memory/decisions/` as a draft (ratify-with-human;
    never silently overwrite the spine).

## Common failure modes to avoid

- Staging a debate or inventing personas. The judgement is the advisor's.
- Marching through six questions when context already answered four.
- Killing a move with no constructive exit.
- Blessing the full build instead of the cheapest proof.
- Counting cost in cash only, ignoring attention and opportunity cost (cost doctrine).
- Calling a verdict on a missing foundation instead of routing to the skill that fills
  it.
- Treating a past decision as settled instead of re-deriving from evidence.
- Letting an off-spine move through because it "would do numbers".

## Method lineage

Use as lineage, not as copy:

- YC office-hours forcing-question discipline (demand reality, status quo, desperate
  specificity, narrowest wedge), adapted from product validation to marketing bets and
  stripped of the panel/pitch apparatus.
- Pre-mortem (Gary Klein): assume the move failed, work back to why, before committing.
- Inference doctrine: strategy before tactics, visible and graded assumptions, the
  objectivity dividend (one disciplined judgement, not a chorus), cost doctrine (attention
  and opportunity cost are real cost), and the cheapest-proof / kill-or-narrow exit shared
  with `growth-bets`.
- Voice: dry mode on spend and bad news; concision; flag once,
  then commit.

## Handoffs

- `growth-bets` — a proceed becomes a bet with a full proof plan; or "what instead" when
  the answer is no.
- `channel-map` — route-to-market detail if the move is a channel.
- `icp` / `positioning` / `growth-model` — close the foundation gap behind a "not yet".
- measurement / instrumentation — when the move fails the visibility gate.
- `agentic-opportunity` — if the move's economics change enough with agents to be worth
  reconsidering.
