## Room: Company Strategy

A high-altitude decision room for questions about where the company is going, not how it markets itself. Convene this room when the answer will shape the shape of the company itself.

## When to use this room

- Strategy pivots and repositioning at the company level (not the marketing level)
- Market entry, market exit, or category bets
- Build / buy / partner decisions
- Org-level bets (new business unit, new GTM model, new pricing architecture)
- "Should we be doing this at all" questions
- Capital allocation decisions where the operating model, the cost structure, and the category story all have to agree — and usually don't

**When *not* to use this room:**

- If the question is really about positioning, naming, messaging, or campaign craft, use the **marketing-agency** room. This room will over-abstract marketing questions and under-serve them.
- If the company is venture-backed *and* the question turns on board dynamics, fundraising posture, or outcome-shape pressure from investors, use the **vc-backed-startup-strategy** room. That room keeps the investor seat in the default roster and is built around the founder/investor tension that defines VC-backed strategy decisions. This room can be used for venture-backed companies, but its default roster doesn't surface the investor lens — swap in `investor-board-proxy` from the bench or switch rooms.
- If the question is tactical execution inside an already-decided strategy, neither room is right — just answer the question.

## Default roster (v1)

Five personas spanning four axes:

1. **founder-ceo-proxy** — role / conviction and long narrative arc *(reused from marketing-agency room)*
2. **operator-coo** — functional / execution horizon
3. **cfo** — functional / capital and margin
4. **category-historian** — worldview / precedents and cycles
5. **contrarian-skeptic** — stance / guaranteed pressure *(reused)*

This roster satisfies the composition rule: 4 axes (functional ×2, role ×1, worldview ×1, stance ×1), 1 stance persona, no two personas doing the same job. The core tensions this roster is designed to generate:

- **CFO vs Founder** — affordability and downside cases vs ambition.
- **Operator vs everyone** — "who actually does this on Monday and what breaks." The anti-fantasy voice.
- **Category Historian vs Founder** — "this has been tried" vs "this time is different." Forces both to be specific.
- **Skeptic vs the whole plan** — guaranteed pressure on whatever the room is converging toward.

## Bench (sub in when the question calls for it)

- **investor-board-proxy** — when the company is venture-backed, has a real board, or the decision has to be defensible to outside capital. If the investor lens is central to the question (fundraising posture, board approval, outcome-shape pressure), strongly consider switching to the **vc-backed-startup-strategy** room rather than just swapping the seat — the framing and load-bearing tensions of that room are different.
- **first-principles-thinker** — when the question is stuck inside an inherited framing and the room needs someone to ask "wait, why is that true?" Especially useful when the founder, the market, or convention has handed the company a story that may not hold up under inspection.
- **end-customer** — when the strategic decision touches product-market reality and the room risks abstracting away from the person whose life the product is supposed to change. *(reused from marketing-agency)*
- **brand-strategist** — when the strategic decision has a multi-year brand-equity dimension and the CFO is about to win the argument on pure payback-window grounds. *(reused from marketing-agency)*

## Swap guidance for the orchestrator

- **"Should we enter market X / launch product line Y?"** — default roster, and consider subbing **end-customer** in for **brand-strategist** if the decision touches a new user type.
- **"Should we build / buy / partner for capability Z?"** — default roster. Operator-COO is doing the heaviest lifting here; brief them to produce a concrete 90-day plan for each option.
- **"Pivot / reposition the whole company?"** — add **first-principles-thinker** and keep the full default roster (drop one seat if you need to stay at six — but only if the question genuinely doesn't touch that seat).
- **"Should this even be a company / are we building the right thing at all?"** — swap **cfo** for **first-principles-thinker**. The question is upstream of capital allocation.
- **"Org design / new business unit / new GTM model?"** — keep the default roster, but brief **operator-coo** to lead phase 2 (their seat is closest to the decision).
- **Capital structure, fundraising posture, or anything where the board's view is load-bearing?** — switch to the **vc-backed-startup-strategy** room; that question is what that room is for.

Always preserve: at least one stance persona (**contrarian-skeptic** by default), at least 3 axes, no duplicates, 4–6 seats.

**Load-bearing seat rule: never drop the seat that is the subject of the question.** If the question is "how do we compete against better-funded rivals," the capital-asymmetry is the *core premise of the question* — keep the CFO regardless of how "strategic" the framing feels, because the whole question is a capital question wearing a strategy costume. Similar principle applies more broadly: if the question is about execution capacity, keep operator-COO. If the question is about a category bet, keep category-historian. The temptation to drop a functional seat because the question "feels strategic" is usually wrong — strategy questions that can be abstracted away from their functional grounding tend to produce recommendations that look sharp in the room and fall apart outside it. When in doubt about which seat is load-bearing, re-read the user's question and ask: *"Which seat's worldview, if absent, would let the room skip the hardest version of the question?"* Keep that seat.

## Notes on running this room

- **Phase 2.5 (rebuttal) is default-on here and especially valuable.** Strategy decisions are where convergence drift hurts most. If the question has specific options (build vs buy; enter market vs not), assign one persona to steelman the losing option.
- **Watch for load-bearing assumptions in phase 3.** Company-strategy conversations lean heavily on unverified claims about market size, competitive behaviour, and team capacity. Name those assumptions, name who made them, mark them unverified.
- **The end customer is often *missing* from strategy rooms and shouldn't be.** If the decision touches the product at all, consider subbing them in or expanding to a 7th seat.
