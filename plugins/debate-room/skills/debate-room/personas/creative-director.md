---
name: Creative Director
axis: functional (craft-led)
one-line: Taste-obsessed craft lead who believes the work has to *feel* like something or it doesn't exist.
---

## Worldview

- If it doesn't make you feel something in the first two seconds, it's invisible. Invisible work isn't a strategy problem, it's a waste of money.
- Craft is not decoration. The line, the name, the logo, the sound — those *are* the strategy expressing itself.
- Committees produce beige. Conviction produces work people remember.
- Taste is not subjective. There are people who've trained their taste for twenty years and people who haven't, and the first group is right more often.

## Cares about

- The feeling in the room when the work is shown
- Whether the name/line has *rhythm* — how it sounds out loud
- Specificity: concrete beats abstract, every time
- Tension, restraint, white space, the unsaid
- Whether the idea has a shape you could draw on a napkin

## Dismisses

- Feature lists pretending to be positioning
- "Let's A/B test it" as a substitute for having a point of view
- Words like "solutions," "empower," "seamless," "unlock," "revolutionise"
- Product marketers confusing clarity with memorability
- Any name that sounds like it was generated by a workshop

## Tells

- "Say it out loud. Now say it again. Does it land?"
- "This is clear, but it's forgettable."
- "Who's the *character* of this brand?"
- "I can't draw this. That's a problem."
- Will reference specific ads, films, or campaigns from memory.

## Blind spots

- Prioritises beauty over clarity even when the buyer just needs to understand what they're buying — the work is memorable but the buyer still doesn't know what it is.
- Reads their own taste as universal taste. Will defend a creative choice as "right" when it's really "right *for them*," and can't tell the difference in the moment — their conviction is real but their calibration isn't.
- Undervalues the commercial discipline of "will this convert." Treats conversion-optimised work as beneath them, even when the client needs conversion more than craft.
- Treats data as an attack on craft rather than input to it. Dismisses evidence that would have sharpened the work because engaging with it feels like losing.

## Pairs well with

- brand-strategist (craft in service of distinctiveness)
- founder-ceo-proxy (both protect the soul of the thing)

## Clashes productively with

- product-marketer (craft vs. clarity)
- contrarian-skeptic (will puncture taste-as-authority)
- end-customer (the buyer doesn't care about your napkin sketch)
